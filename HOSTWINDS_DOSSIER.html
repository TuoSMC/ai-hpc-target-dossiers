1. Bottom line

Hostwinds LLC is a privately held Tukwila (Seattle-metro), Washington bare-metal, VPS, cloud and reseller-hosting provider founded in Tulsa, Oklahoma, running its own ASN (AS54290, 303,360 IPv4 addresses) out of three leased colocation sites — Seattle/Tukwila, Dallas and Amsterdam — plus resold colocation capacity in seven further markets. 10. Cost ceiling

10.1 Rent-derived hardware ceiling

Method. Ceiling at N months = (list monthly price × contribution margin) × N, where contribution margin = 1 minus the operating-cost deduction. Annual revenue = list monthly price × 12. All ceilings below are computed at a 50% contribution margin, i.e. a 50% operating-cost deduction.

THE OPERATING-COST DEDUCTION IS AN ASSUMPTION, NOT A RESEARCHED FACT. Hostwinds' and ColoCrossing's actual power tariffs, transit contracts, rack costs and support cost per server were not obtained, and none of the components below were verified against any source. They are industry rules of thumb for a US legacy-Xeon colocation fleet and must be labelled as such in any customer-facing use.

ASSUMPTION BAND — total operating-cost deduction 40% to 60% of list price, midpoint 50%, built from:

  • Power — assumed, not verified. Single-socket 1U legacy Xeon E3 at roughly 100–150 W average draw is about 0.11 kW; at $0.09–$0.14/kWh with a PUE of 1.4–1.6 that is roughly $11–$22/month. Dual-socket E5 boxes at 250–350 W are roughly $25–$50/month. Assumed band per server: $10–$50.
  • Bandwidth — assumed, not verified. 40 TB outbound is included in every SKU. At wholesale blended transit of roughly $0.30–$0.80 per Mbps per month, and with typical dedicated-server customers using well under their allowance, assumed band per server: $5–$25/month. Overage tiers are priced separately by Hostwinds ($150 for 60 TB up to $750 unmetered) and are excluded from this calculation.
  • Rack, space and cross-connect — assumed, not verified. Amortised 1U slot cost in owned or long-leased space, assumed band: $12–$30/month. Note Hostwinds' own retail colocation prices a 42U/3.84 kW cabinet at $1,399/month, which implies roughly $33/U/month at retail — the internal cost is materially lower than that, which is why the assumed band sits below it.
  • Support, provisioning, billing, payment processing, IP/ARIN costs, churn and remarketing — assumed, not verified. Assumed band: 15–25% of list price. Hostwinds markets these as "fully managed" servers with 24/7/365 support, so this component is real and non-trivial.

SENSITIVITY. At the optimistic end of the band (40% deduction, 60% contribution) every ceiling below rises by 20%; at the pessimistic end (60% deduction, 40% contribution) every ceiling falls by 20%. For the volume SKU (E3-1270 v2 at $90/month) the 12-month hardware ceiling therefore spans $432 to $648 around the $540 midpoint.

MATERIAL CAVEATS TO CARRY.

  1. Ceilings are computed on BASE price only. RAM is metered separately at a flat $1/GB/month, which is close to pure margin against a street cost of roughly $2.80/GB for used DDR4-2133 RDIMM — a 32 GB upsell adds $32/month, or $768 over 24 months, against a one-off parts cost of about $90. Bandwidth overages and additional IP blocks are also incremental. A realistically configured customer therefore pays well above base, so these ceilings are conservative floors.
  2. Ceilings assume 100% occupancy for the full payback window. Hostwinds currently advertises 405 IDLE dedicated units, so real fleet-level occupancy is demonstrably below 100% and the effective ceiling per rack unit is lower than shown.
  3. A 12-month payback is the aggressive hosting-industry norm; 24–36 months is common for stable enterprise-facing capacity. Given this fleet is running 2007–2017 silicon that was almost certainly bought at deep secondary-market discounts, actual historical paybacks were probably far shorter than any of these windows.

GAP — operating-cost deduction is unverified. The 40–60% band (power, bandwidth, rack, support) is an industry rule of thumb. No Hostwinds or ColoCrossing power tariff, transit contract, rack cost or support cost per server was obtained. Every ceiling figure moves ±20% across the band.

What the gap means for pitching an integrated platform against self-assembly.

The honest read is uncomfortable and should be stated plainly to the deal team: on a pure parts-versus-ceiling arithmetic, self-assembly from the secondary market wins overwhelmingly on this fleet. A box that rents for $90/month is built from $260 of used parts. No new integrated platform can compete with $4.95 CPUs on acquisition price. Any pitch that tries to win on hardware cost per box against this incumbent behaviour will lose, and lose badly.

The gap that actually matters is not price, it is the four costs the BOM does not contain and the secondary market cannot supply.

  1. Density and power. A 42U cabinet at 3.84 kW — Hostwinds' own published colocation spec — will hold roughly 25–38 of these 1U legacy boxes before the power budget is exhausted, delivering perhaps 100–150 aging cores per cabinet. A single modern dual-socket node consolidates that entire cabinet into 2U. When ColoCrossing is opening new Buffalo floor space, the binding constraint is kilowatts and square feet, not dollars per CPU, and that is the axis where an integrated platform wins by an order of magnitude.
  2. The memory tax. Hostwinds meters RAM at a flat $1/GB/month while used DDR4-2133 costs roughly $2.80/GB to buy — a 30-month payback on memory alone. Modern platforms with far higher DIMM capacity per socket turn that same metering into materially more revenue per rack unit. The memory upsell, not the base server, is where the margin lives, and the legacy platform caps it.
  3. The product gap. The catalogue tops out at 3 TB HDD or 1 TB SATA SSD. There is no NVMe anywhere, no GPU SKU has ever existed, and the newest CPU on offer is a 2017 Kaby Lake quad-core. Meanwhile the group has just launched a ten-market colocation product and a "Quote Request" enterprise motion. You cannot sell enterprise colocation and AI-adjacent capacity from a fleet whose flagship is a $200/month dual E5-2620 v3. The competitive pressure is not from cheaper parts, it is from every rival selling NVMe and current-generation cores.
  4. Operational cost per box. Self-assembly from mixed brokered stock produces a fleet with no common firmware baseline, no vendor support path, no unified management plane, and a hardware failure rate consistent with 9-to-19-year-old components. That is invisible in a BOM and enormous in an operating budget — and it is precisely the cost that consolidating three acquired estates into one facility exposes.

Therefore the sales argument must be reframed from cost-per-server to revenue-per-kilowatt and revenue-per-rack-unit, aimed at ColoCrossing's Buffalo expansion decision and funded from the $130M BMO facility — not at Hostwinds' historical procurement habit, which no integrated platform will ever out-price.

10. 成本天花板

10.1 由租金推導的硬體天花板

方法。 N 個月的天花板 ＝（月定價 × 貢獻率）× N，其中貢獻率 ＝ 1 減去營運成本扣減率。年營收 ＝ 月定價 × 12。以下所有天花板皆以 50% 貢獻率計算，亦即扣減 50% 營運成本。

營運成本扣減率是「假設」，不是查證所得的事實。 本次並未取得 Hostwinds 或 ColoCrossing 實際的電費費率、傳輸合約、機櫃成本或每台伺服器的支援成本，下列各項組成皆未經任何來源驗證。它們是美國舊世代 Xeon colocation 機隊的業界經驗法則，任何對客戶使用的場合都必須如此標示。

假設區間——營運成本扣減率合計 40% 至 60%，中點 50%，組成如下：

  • 電力——假設值，未經驗證。 單路 1U 舊世代 Xeon E3 平均約 100–150 W，約 0.11 kW；以每度 $0.09–$0.14、PUE 1.4–1.6 計，約為每月 $11–$22。雙路 E5 機種 250–350 W，約為每月 $25–$50。每台假設區間：$10–$50。
  • 頻寬——假設值，未經驗證。 每個 SKU 都含 40 TB 對外流量。以批發混合傳輸價每 Mbps 每月約 $0.30–$0.80、且典型專用伺服器客戶用量遠低於額度計，每台假設區間：每月 $5–$25。超量級距由 Hostwinds 另行計價（60 TB $150 起，至不限流量 $750），已排除於本計算之外。
  • 機櫃、空間與 cross-connect——假設值，未經驗證。 自有或長租空間攤提後的 1U 位置成本，假設區間：每月 $12–$30。注意 Hostwinds 自家零售 colocation 對 42U／3.84 kW 整櫃訂價為每月 $1,399，換算零售約為每 U 每月 $33——內部成本明顯低於此，這也是假設區間設在其下方的原因。
  • 支援、開通、帳務、金流、IP／ARIN 成本、流失與再銷售——假設值，未經驗證。 假設區間：定價的 15–25%。Hostwinds 以「fully managed」加 24/7/365 支援行銷這些機器，因此這一塊是真實且不可忽略的成本。

敏感度。 取區間樂觀端（扣減 40%、貢獻 60%），以下每一個天花板都上升 20%；取悲觀端（扣減 60%、貢獻 40%），每一個天花板都下降 20%。以量最大的 SKU（E3-1270 v2，每月 $90）為例，12 個月硬體天花板因此落在中點 $540 附近的 $432 至 $648 區間。

這個落差對「整合平台 vs 自行組裝」的提案代表什麼。

誠實的解讀並不舒服，而且應該對交易團隊明講：單就「料件 vs 天花板」的算術而言，在這支機隊上，從二手市場自行組裝是壓倒性勝出的。 一台月租 $90 的機器，是用 $260 的二手料件組成的。沒有任何新的整合平台能在購置價格上跟 $4.95 的 CPU 競爭。任何試圖以「每台硬體成本」去對打這種既有採購行為的提案，都會輸，而且會輸得很難看。

真正重要的落差不在價格，而在料表沒有包含、且二手市場也無法供給的四項成本。

  1. 密度與電力。 一個 3.84 kW 的 42U 機櫃——這是 Hostwinds 自家公布的 colocation 規格——在電力額度用盡前大約只能裝 25–38 台這種 1U 舊機，每櫃大約提供 100–150 顆老核心。一台現代雙路節點就能把整櫃壓縮進 2U。當 ColoCrossing 正在開出新的 Buffalo 機房空間時，約束條件是千瓦與坪數，不是每顆 CPU 多少錢——而那正是整合平台以數量級勝出的軸線。
  2. 記憶體稅。 Hostwinds 對 RAM 以固定 $1/GB/月計價，而二手 DDR4-2133 的購入成本約 $2.80/GB——光記憶體就是 30 個月回本。現代平台每路可插的 DIMM 容量高出許多，同樣的計價方式就能轉化成每個 U 明顯更高的營收。毛利活在記憶體加購上，不在基本機器上，而舊平台把這塊上限鎖死了。
  3. 產品落差。 型錄的天花板是 3 TB HDD 或 1 TB SATA SSD。全線沒有 NVMe、從來沒有出現過 GPU SKU，最新的 CPU 是 2017 年的 Kaby Lake 四核。 與此同時，集團才剛推出十市場 colocation 產品與「Quote Request」的企業級動線。你不可能用一支旗艦是每月 $200 雙路 E5-2620 v3 的機隊，去賣企業級 colocation 與 AI 相鄰的運算容量。 競爭壓力不是來自更便宜的料件，而是來自每一個在賣 NVMe 與現世代核心的對手。
  4. 每台的營運成本。 用混雜的掮客貨自行組裝，得到的是沒有統一韌體基準、沒有原廠支援路徑、沒有統一管理平面的機隊，加上與 9 到 19 年老元件相符的硬體故障率。這在料表上完全看不見，在營運預算上卻極為龐大——而這正是把三批被收購資產整併進同一座機房時，會被暴露出來的成本。

因此，銷售論述必須從「每台伺服器成本」重新框成「每千瓦營收」與「每 U 營收」，瞄準 ColoCrossing 的 Buffalo 擴建決策、由 1.3 億美元 BMO 額度支應——而不是去對打 Hostwinds 的歷史採購習慣，那是任何整合平台都不可能在價格上勝過的。

分類：全新開發（greenfield）AI——附帶條件，且採購權可能已移轉。 這不是既有客戶防守案（無任何已確認 OEM），不是替換案（無任何具名競爭廠商），也不是 WebNX 那種平台轉換案。這是新客開發，並卡在兩件事上。
