## 1. Bottom line

ReliableSite is a founder-owned, ~11–50-person U.S. bare-metal / dedicated-server provider running its own AS23470 network and its own servers across six leased colocation regions — Piscataway NJ, Newark NJ, Miami FL, Los Angeles CA, Amsterdam NL, and a Querétaro MX build-out — selling single-socket desktop/workstation-class machines at $49–$699/month. The commercial verdict is that this is a **confirmed Supermicro account that has already been half-lost**: the chassis business is gone to an in-house design, but the motherboard and power-supply socket is explicitly still open, and there is a clean, empty AI/GPU shelf to fill — so this is a board-level defence plus a greenfield GPU play, not a routine renewal, and it must be run by the East Coast team, not Team 1. The single strongest piece of evidence in the whole file is CEO Radic Davydov's on-record [LowEndBox interview](https://lowendbox.com/blog/custom-built-for-success-interview-with-radic-davydov-ceo-of-reliablesite/), in which he names Supermicro MicroCloud in his fleet and then explains, in his own words, that he designed a proprietary ~50%-higher-density chassis after MicroCloud replacement parts became unavailable and cost "2-3 times the cost" — while deliberately keeping it compatible with off-the-shelf motherboards and power supplies. The one thing that could kill the deal is opening with a vendor-integrated chassis or complete-system pitch: that re-opens the exact wound that lost the account, and the honest opening is the board/PSU socket and the missing GPU platform — the motherboard supplier behind the custom chassis is the single most commercially important unknown in this entire dossier.

## 6. Hardware fleet

Evidence grades used here: **CONFIRMED** = first-hand named disclosure by the operator, or multiple independent corroborating sources · **CIRCUMSTANTIAL** = behaviour strongly implies it but it is never named · **INFERRED** = derived only from CPU/chassis shape · **CONTRADICTED** = evidence runs against the working-list claim · **GAP** = not found.

- **Supermicro — CONFIRMED. This is the strongest hardware evidence in the whole account, and it must always be delivered together with the next bullet.**
  Systems: Supermicro barebones with onboard IPMI/KVM; **Supermicro MicroCloud** multi-node chassis named explicitly by the CEO. **Exact SKUs are not published.**
  Three independent strands: (1) a Web Hosting Talk customer review stating that all of that customer's ReliableSite servers "use Supermicro hardware with onboard KVM"; (2) a separate WHT thread describing the purchase of three ReliableSite dedicated servers as "Supermicro barebones, Atom D510, 4GB, 500GB" — community-sourced, but two separate threads corroborate; (3) operator-level confirmation — CEO Radic Davydov discusses Supermicro MicroCloud servers and the difficulty of replacing failed parts in the LowEndBox interview.
  **Limitation to state honestly: the WHT Atom D510 reference is legacy-era hardware, and current-generation SKU-level confirmation is a GAP** ([Web Hosting Talk](https://www.webhostingtalk.com/showthread.php?t=1640888); [LowEndBox interview](https://lowendbox.com/blog/custom-built-for-success-interview-with-radic-davydov-ceo-of-reliablesite/)).

- **In-house / custom chassis (whitebox) — CONFIRMED, and this is the key competitive dynamic in the account.**
  A proprietary ReliableSite-designed server chassis achieving **~50% higher density than standard 1U**, deliberately built to accept **off-the-shelf motherboards and power supplies**; a prototype targeting a 100% density improvement was in development as of the interview.
  The CEO's own on-record explanation: he designed the proprietary chassis after Supermicro MicroCloud replacement parts became unavailable or expensive — "These failed parts could not be replaced with newer generation parts, even though they were nearly identical, and were 2-3 times the cost." That is an explicit, first-person reason for moving **away** from a vendor-integrated chassis toward custom sheet metal plus merchant motherboards. Company marketing reinforces it: "each server built in-house with enterprise grade hardware" and "custom hardware design and manufacturing."
  **Sales implication: the chassis is theirs, but the motherboard / board-level socket is open** ([LowEndBox interview](https://lowendbox.com/blog/custom-built-for-success-interview-with-radic-davydov-ceo-of-reliablesite/)).

## 11. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **confirmed incumbent — but in practice this is a chassis-loss defence in which the board-level socket is still open, plus a greenfield AI/GPU play.**

This is **not** a routine renewal and it is **not** a cold displacement. Supermicro is genuinely in this fleet, confirmed by three independent strands (§6). But the same CEO interview that confirms it is also the on-record account of **why they walked away from the vendor chassis**: MicroCloud replacement parts became unavailable, near-identical newer-generation parts would not fit, and the price was "2-3 times the cost", so he built his own ~50%-denser chassis — **while deliberately keeping it compatible with off-the-shelf motherboards and power supplies.**

That single design decision defines the entire honest wedge:

1. **Board and PSU, not chassis.** The chassis business is gone and should not be re-pitched first — re-opening it re-opens the grievance. What is still open, by the CEO's own published design intent, is the **motherboard and power supply socket**. **Who supplies the boards for the custom chassis is the single most commercially important unknown in this dossier**, and it is unanswerable from public sources.
2. **Parts availability and lead time is a live, first-hand pain point.** The CEO publicly stated an **"800% cost increase"** on specific SSD/RAM parts with **3+ month lead times** ([LowEndTalk](https://lowendtalk.com/discussion/218739/us-eu-dedicated-servers-epyc-4545p-ryzen-5800x-intel-core-i9-9900k-more-new-metal-benefits)). Long-life part availability and a stable supply commitment speak directly to the reason he left in the first place.
3. **The AI gap is real and published.** They market "GPU AI Dedicated Servers", position the EPYC 4545P for AI inferencing, and publish an AI-vision customer case study — with **one A10 (QTY 1) and a $99/month Quadro RTX 4000 add-on** behind it. A 4-GPU L40S / RTX PRO class node they can list, price and self-serve is the natural first GPU SKU, sized to a 1–2 rack single-socket operation, **not** an HGX/SXM scale-out pitch.
4. **They buy platforms Dell and HPE do not build.** The fleet is single-socket desktop/workstation silicon — AM5 Ryzen 9950X, LGA1700 i9 14900K, Threadripper 7995WX, EPYC 4545P. That is a structural product fit, and it is why there is no Dell or HPE incumbency to displace (both = no evidence found).
5. **Querétaro is the timing trigger.** The QRO region is publicly "coming soon" with the network test not yet live — a build-out in progress is the most likely near-term new-hardware purchase event in the account.

### The qualifying question to ask on first contact

> **"What motherboard and power supply are you using in your own chassis — and what platform is going into Querétaro?"**

Ask this and nothing else on the first call. It resolves the highest-value unknown in the dossier in one sentence, it acknowledges their custom chassis as theirs (which respects the design decision rather than attacking it), and it surfaces the live build-out. **Do not quote. Do not assume an incumbent. Do not open with "we know you run Supermicro"** — that is true of the fleet's history but is not confirmed at current-generation SKU level, and the CEO's own published account of leaving MicroCloud means a possessive opening will land badly.

### Rule 8 distributor caution — read before anyone dials

This provider runs a sizeable fleet — six regions, ~50,944 IPv4 addresses, five IXP presences on 100G ports, 1–5 Tbps self-reported — **and has never once appeared in the CRM** (verified 2026-08-03 in salesleads Search, Type = All: no lead, no account, no do-not-call record). The only reasonable inference is that **the servers are bought through distribution, not direct from the manufacturer.** The corroborating signal is their own stated posture: they "keep our inventory on site" and go "from tray to rack" themselves, which is exactly the behaviour of a component- and barebones-level buyer sourcing through a channel partner.

**Under Rule 8, a distributor may cross territory boundaries, but this requires advance approval and a do-not-call flag.** Therefore: **confirm the channel first, register the lead second, make contact last.** Before anyone places the first call, check with the channel desk whether ReliableSite is already an existing account of a distributor. If it is, this file changes from new-business development to a distributor-co-managed existing account, and the opening, the quoting path and the registration method are all different. **Reversing that order creates channel conflict.**

**Territory sequencing:** Team 1 cannot work this account alone. FL = East Coast 3 and NJ = East Coast 1, both **T2 | T3 | T6 | T7 | T12**; the Large End User / Data Center exception does not apply because **Rule 11 sets the threshold at 100MW or more** and this provider is far below it. **Issue the co-op request to T7 (lead: Brian Leaver).** T11 is not eligible and should not receive this file.
