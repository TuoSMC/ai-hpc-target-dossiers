## 1. Bottom line

phoenixNAP (legally **PHOENIX NAP, LLC**, an Arizona domestic LLC, ACC file **L15102933**, entity status ACTIVE) is a privately held global Bare Metal Cloud, dedicated-server and colocation operator headquartered at its own flagship datacenter at 3402 East University Drive, Phoenix AZ 85034, running **AS12189** plus seven further ASNs, with priced compute in six regions and network presence in sixteen facilities across four continents ([GLEIF LEI 549300AC0LNX8QT0G149](https://api.gleif.org/api/v1/lei-records/549300AC0LNX8QT0G149), validated at the Arizona Corporation Commission; [PeeringDB AS12189](https://www.peeringdb.com/api/net?asn=12189&depth=2)). Commercially this is **an incumbent-defence account with an urgent displacement threat, and anyone who walks in treating it as greenfield will lose it.** Supermicro is the documented nine-year incumbent — Supermicro published a joint case study with phoenixNAP in June 2017 quoting President Ian McClarty and VP of Products William Bell by name, covering X11 Building Block Solutions, BigTwin 2U 4-node systems, Simply Double all-flash SuperStorage, Supermicro Rack Scale Design and Supermicro Server Manager across all geographies ([CaseStudy_PhoenixNAP.pdf](https://www.supermicro.com/CaseStudies/CaseStudy_PhoenixNAP.pdf)) — and ServeTheHome physically identified a phoenixNAP production node as a Supermicro twin node as recently as March 2023 ([ServeTheHome, 2023-03-31](https://www.servethehome.com/putting-the-bare-metal-server-in-the-phoenixnap-bare-metal-cloud-intel-xeon-sapphire-rapids-supermicro/)). **But in April 2025 HPE publicly claimed phoenixNAP as the site of its first DC-MHS-conforming disaggregated hardware deployment on Intel Xeon 6** ([HPE newsroom, April 2025](https://www.hpe.com/us/en/newsroom/press-release/2025/04/phoenixnap-advances-cloud-services-using-hpe-disaggregated-data-center-modular-hardware-system-servers-with-intel-xeon-6.html)), and the matching `s4.x6` Xeon 6 6731E SKUs appear in phoenixNAP's own catalogue in exactly that window. A competitor has planted a flag on the newest silicon inside a Supermicro account.

Three facts make the timing unusually good. First, **they own their fleet** — this is not an inference: the Arizona Tax Court record states as undisputed fact that IaaS customers use "PNAP-owned servers" and the Court found the servers are tangible personal property PNAP rents out ([TX2024-000075 minute entry, filed 2026-05-21](https://superiorcourt.maricopa.gov/media/yvoj0ksi/tx2024-000075.pdf)). Second, **a next-generation GPU SKU has been staged and stalled for sixteen months**: product code `d3.g3.c2.medium` exists in phoenixNAP's own product registry with no hardware spec, no GPU model and no price, visible only as a $0 correlate on two Windows Server 2025 licence rows — present in the Wayback snapshot cached 2025-04-22 and still present, unchanged, in the live catalogue on 2026-08-11 ([phoenixNAP cached pricing API](https://phoenixnap.com/wp-content/themes/bootscore-child/cache/api-data.json)). That is a GPU platform decision made in principle and not yet closed on silicon or capex. Third, **on 12 March 2026 RadiusDC agreed to buy the Phoenix datacenter and colocation business**, closing Q2 2026, after which phoenixNAP becomes a tenant in its own flagship building and retains roughly 80% of its global business — explicitly Bare Metal Cloud and network ([RadiusDC release, 2026-03-12](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/radiusdc-to-acquire-phoenixnaps-phoenix-data-center-and-colocation-business-302711634.html); [phoenixNAP release, 2026-03-17](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/phoenixnap-sharpens-focus-on-on-demand-infrastructure-with-strategic-transaction-for-phoenix-colocation-business-302716069.html)). They have just converted a real-estate business into a compute business with cash in hand; the fleet is now the entire company.

The thing that could break the deal is price, and it is arithmetic rather than attitude. The rent-derived cost ceiling on their flagship GPU node lands around **$8,000–$12,000** against a street BOM of roughly **$14,000–$18,300** (§10) — those two numbers only reconcile if phoenixNAP buys materially below street, which is exactly what Supermicro's own 2017 case study documents, since phoenixNAP reached Intel's Early Deployment programme *through* Supermicro. The defence of this account runs through the Intel programme relationship and programme pricing, not through chassis features. One more headwind to brief the team on before the first quote: the 18 May 2026 Arizona Tax Court ruling makes their colocation and server-rental revenue prospectively subject to state, county and City of Phoenix transaction privilege tax they were not previously paying, which will make them harder on price this cycle.

What one flagship GPU node can be worth to phoenixNAP, and what the same box costs to build today. **Target: `d3.g2.c3.xlarge`.**

### 10.1 Assumptions — read these first; they are assumptions, not findings

**Only one input below is theirs. Everything else is mine.**

The **published revenue per node is verified** from phoenixNAP's own catalogue ([api-data.json](https://phoenixnap.com/wp-content/themes/bootscore-child/cache/api-data.json), 2026-08-11): **$2.67/hr · $1,778.49/mo at 1 month · $1,110.27/mo at 12 months · $992.12/mo at 24 months · $920.23/mo at 36 months.** From there:

- **ASSUMPTION 1 — sustained revenue rate.** GPU nodes are assumed to sell predominantly on **12-to-36-month reservations** rather than at the 1-month rack rate, giving a sustained band of **$920–$1,110/mo per node**.
- **ASSUMPTION 2 — operating-cost share of 40–55% of revenue.** Covering power at 600 W+ per node (two 300 W accelerators plus dual Sapphire Rapids), cooling, space, network, remote hands, support, SG&A, and — newly, post-May 2026 — **Arizona TPT on server rentals**. **This is the single most load-bearing assumption and there is no phoenixNAP cost data behind it.** It yields a contribution margin of **$414–$666 per node per month**.
- **ASSUMPTION 3 — utilisation is high enough that reserved revenue is continuous.** **GPU nodes sitting idle destroy the whole calculation, and there is no utilisation data.**
- **ASSUMPTION 4 — payback windows of 12 / 18 / 24 months**, reflecting how an owner-operator of this size thinks about GPU depreciation. A 36- or 48-month view would raise the ceiling proportionally.
- **ASSUMPTION 5 — no residual value credited at end of life.**
- **ASSUMPTION 6 — Arizona TPT now applies prospectively to server rentals**, reducing net revenue by a few percent from mid-2026 ([TX2024-000075](https://superiorcourt.maricopa.gov/media/yvoj0ksi/tx2024-000075.pdf)).

**None of these are phoenixNAP-disclosed figures. Every ceiling below is a modelled estimate and must be labelled as such if it leaves this document.**
