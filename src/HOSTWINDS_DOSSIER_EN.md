## 1. Bottom line

Hostwinds LLC is a privately held Tukwila (Seattle-metro), Washington bare-metal, VPS, cloud and reseller-hosting provider founded in Tulsa, Oklahoma, running its own ASN (AS54290, 303,360 IPv4 addresses) out of three leased colocation sites — Seattle/Tukwila, Dallas and Amsterdam — plus resold colocation capacity in seven further markets. The commercial verdict is **greenfield AI with a purchasing-authority question attached**: there is no GPU product of any kind, the sellable dedicated fleet is roughly 8–19 years old with no Xeon Scalable and no EPYC anywhere in it, but on 2026-04-29 the company was acquired by HostPapa, Inc., so capex authority may already have moved north before any refresh conversation can start. The single strongest piece of evidence is the live public order form at [clients.hostwinds.com/cart.php?gid=12](https://clients.hostwinds.com/cart.php?gid=12), which sells 20 CPU-only SKUs spanning Xeon 5300 Clovertown (2007) through E3-1270 v6 Kaby Lake (2017), with a desktop i7-3930K in the rack and not one Xeon Scalable or EPYC part — a fully priced, verifiable generational gap. The one thing that could kill the deal is the acquisition itself: [HostPapa's 2026-04-29 press release](https://www.hostpapa.com/about-hostpapa/pressroom/2026-04-29-hostpapa-acquires-hostwinds-to-expand-global-hosting-infrastructure/) disclosed no terms, no retention commitment for founder Peter Holden and no capex plan, and [ARIN RDAP for AS54290](https://rdap.arin.net/registry/autnum/54290) was re-registered to HostPapa (Buffalo, NY) on 2026-05-12 — if Hostwinds no longer buys its own hardware, every specification discussion held with Tukwila is wasted.

### 9.2 Cadence

**Observed intervals.** Two clean intervals between the three observable purchase events. **Purchase event #1:** E5-2620 v3 (Haswell-EP) first observed 2022-05-09 (true event lies in the window 2021-11-08 → 2022-05-09). **Purchase event #2:** E3-1270 v5 (Skylake) first observed 2023-04-17 (window 2022-05-09 → 2023-04-17) → interval **11.3 months**. **Purchase event #3:** E3-1270 v6 (Kaby Lake) first observed 2024-04-12 (window 2024-01-27 → 2024-04-12) → interval **11.9 months**. Since 2024-04-12: **28 months with ZERO new CPU generations.** Every SKU added since is 2007–2014 silicon.

**Median months.** **11.6 months** (mean of the two observed intervals 11.3 and 11.9; with n=2 the median and mean coincide). Call it a **~12-month refresh rhythm through 2024**.

**Last purchase event.** **2024-04-12 — Intel Xeon E3-1270 v6 (Kaby Lake, launched Q1 2017)** first appears in the dedicated catalogue. That remains the newest silicon Hostwinds offers as of 2026-08-10. **No GPU purchase event has ever been observed: the catalogue has never contained a GPU SKU.**

**Next window estimate.** On the pre-acquisition ~12-month rhythm the next refresh was due around **2025-04** and is now **roughly 16 months overdue — the cadence has broken, not merely slipped**. The decision right no longer sits with Hostwinds. Realistic next window: **H2 2026 to H1 2027**, executed as a **HostPapa-group purchase into ColoCrossing** rather than a Hostwinds purchase. The three converging triggers are **(a)** the [USD $130M BMO senior secured facility closed 2025-06-03](https://www.hostpapa.com/about-hostpapa/pressroom/2025-06-03-hostpapa-closes-usd-130-million-senior-secured-credit-facility/) with an uncommitted accordion, explicitly raised to fund innovation and M&A; **(b)** [ColoCrossing's multi-phase Buffalo data-centre expansion launching Q1 2026](https://www.colocrossing.com/blog/colocrossing-buffalo-data-center-space/), which creates floor space that needs filling; and **(c)** three fleets absorbed in 34 months (Hostopia/Deluxe 2023, Tailor Made Servers 2026-04-17, Hostwinds 2026-04-29) whose combined estate is end-of-life and whose consolidation ColoCrossing has publicly been handed. The new [10-market Hostwinds colocation product](https://www.hostwinds.com/colocation) launched between 2026-05-04 and 2026-08-10 is the demand-side pull: **you cannot sell modern colocation and dedicated capacity off a 2012 Ivy Bridge fleet.**

**Confidence: MEDIUM-LOW on the cadence, LOW on the next window.** Three hard caveats. **First**, n=2 intervals is a thin base for a median. **Second, and more important, this catalogue is a RESIDUAL-INVENTORY view:** it lists units currently unsold, so genuinely new hardware that sells immediately may never surface here, and the true purchase date always precedes first catalogue appearance by an unknown lag. **Third**, this endpoint covers **dedicated servers only** — the VPS/cloud fleet silicon is invisible to it, so "no new dedicated generations" is strong evidence about the dedicated estate and only weak evidence about the cloud estate. **Counterweight supporting the finding:** idle dedicated inventory rose monotonically from 153 units (2021-06) to 405 units (2026-08), a **165% increase**, which is the signature of a shrinking dedicated book, not of a fleet being churned by fresh purchases.

Roughly three snapshots per year could not be met for 2025 or 2026 from this endpoint. **Purchase events falling inside the 2024-12-08 → 2025-12-20 window (12.4 months) and the 2025-12-20 → 2026-08-10 window (7.7 months) could have occurred and been missed** if the hardware sold out before a capture.

## 10. Cost ceiling

### 10.1 Rent-derived hardware ceiling

**Method.** Ceiling at N months = (list monthly price × contribution margin) × N, where contribution margin = 1 minus the operating-cost deduction. Annual revenue = list monthly price × 12. **All ceilings below are computed at a 50% contribution margin, i.e. a 50% operating-cost deduction.**

> **THE OPERATING-COST DEDUCTION IS AN ASSUMPTION, NOT A RESEARCHED FACT.** Hostwinds' and ColoCrossing's actual power tariffs, transit contracts, rack costs and support cost per server were not obtained, and **none of the components below were verified against any source**. They are industry rules of thumb for a US legacy-Xeon colocation fleet and **must be labelled as such in any customer-facing use**.

**ASSUMPTION BAND — total operating-cost deduction 40% to 60% of list price, midpoint 50%**, built from:

- **Power — assumed, not verified.** Single-socket 1U legacy Xeon E3 at roughly 100–150 W average draw is about 0.11 kW; at $0.09–$0.14/kWh with a PUE of 1.4–1.6 that is roughly **$11–$22/month**. Dual-socket E5 boxes at 250–350 W are roughly **$25–$50/month**. Assumed band per server: **$10–$50**.
- **Bandwidth — assumed, not verified.** 40 TB outbound is included in every SKU. At wholesale blended transit of roughly $0.30–$0.80 per Mbps per month, and with typical dedicated-server customers using well under their allowance, assumed band per server: **$5–$25/month**. Overage tiers are priced separately by Hostwinds ($150 for 60 TB up to $750 unmetered) and are **excluded** from this calculation.
- **Rack, space and cross-connect — assumed, not verified.** Amortised 1U slot cost in owned or long-leased space, assumed band: **$12–$30/month**. Note Hostwinds' own retail colocation prices a 42U/3.84 kW cabinet at $1,399/month, which implies roughly **$33/U/month at retail** — the internal cost is materially lower than that, which is why the assumed band sits below it.
- **Support, provisioning, billing, payment processing, IP/ARIN costs, churn and remarketing — assumed, not verified.** Assumed band: **15–25% of list price**. Hostwinds markets these as "fully managed" servers with 24/7/365 support, so this component is real and non-trivial.

**SENSITIVITY.** At the optimistic end of the band (40% deduction, 60% contribution) **every ceiling below rises by 20%**; at the pessimistic end (60% deduction, 40% contribution) **every ceiling falls by 20%**. For the volume SKU (E3-1270 v2 at $90/month) the 12-month hardware ceiling therefore spans **$432 to $648** around the $540 midpoint.

**MATERIAL CAVEATS TO CARRY.**

1. **Ceilings are computed on BASE price only.** RAM is metered separately at a flat **$1/GB/month**, which is close to pure margin against a street cost of roughly $2.80/GB for used DDR4-2133 RDIMM — a 32 GB upsell adds $32/month, or **$768 over 24 months**, against a one-off parts cost of about $90. Bandwidth overages and additional IP blocks are also incremental. **A realistically configured customer therefore pays well above base, so these ceilings are conservative floors.**
2. **Ceilings assume 100% occupancy for the full payback window.** Hostwinds currently advertises **405 IDLE dedicated units**, so real fleet-level occupancy is demonstrably below 100% and the effective ceiling per rack unit is lower than shown.
3. **A 12-month payback is the aggressive hosting-industry norm**; 24–36 months is common for stable enterprise-facing capacity. Given this fleet is running 2007–2017 silicon that was almost certainly bought at deep secondary-market discounts, **actual historical paybacks were probably far shorter than any of these windows**.

**GAP — operating-cost deduction is unverified.** The 40–60% band (power, bandwidth, rack, support) is an industry rule of thumb. **No Hostwinds or ColoCrossing power tariff, transit contract, rack cost or support cost per server was obtained. Every ceiling figure moves ±20% across the band.**

**BOM 1 — E3-1270 v2 (the volume SKU, 209 idle units, $90/mo).** Assembled component cost: CPU $23.69 + motherboard $29.00 + 8 GB ECC $24.99 + 1 TB HDD $24.99 + NIC $0 (onboard) + 1U chassis/PSU $156.99 = **$259.66**. **BOM 2 — 2 × E5-2620 v3 (top of range, $200/mo).** Assembled: CPUs $9.90 + motherboard $99.99 + 64 GB DDR4 $180.00 + 1 TB HDD $24.99 + chassis/PSU $156.99 (4-bay proxy standing in for an 8-bay) = **$471.87**. Self-assembly saves roughly **$167 per box, about 26%**, and that saving buys the operator warranty-free, iDRAC-less, unsupported hardware. Note again where the money sits: the two 12-core-equivalent CPUs cost **$9.90 combined**, while the memory costs **$180.00 — eighteen times the silicon.**

Even with those two lines added at plausible cost the build lands comfortably inside the **$810** twelve-month ceiling.

**What the gap means for pitching an integrated platform against self-assembly.**

The honest read is uncomfortable and should be stated plainly to the deal team: **on a pure parts-versus-ceiling arithmetic, self-assembly from the secondary market wins overwhelmingly on this fleet.** A box that rents for $90/month is built from $260 of used parts. No new integrated platform can compete with $4.95 CPUs on acquisition price. **Any pitch that tries to win on hardware cost per box against this incumbent behaviour will lose, and lose badly.**

The gap that actually matters is not price, it is the **four costs the BOM does not contain** and the secondary market cannot supply.

1. **Density and power.** A 42U cabinet at 3.84 kW — Hostwinds' own [published colocation spec](https://www.hostwinds.com/colocation) — will hold roughly **25–38** of these 1U legacy boxes before the power budget is exhausted, delivering perhaps **100–150 aging cores per cabinet**. A single modern dual-socket node consolidates that entire cabinet into 2U. When ColoCrossing is opening new Buffalo floor space, **the binding constraint is kilowatts and square feet, not dollars per CPU**, and that is the axis where an integrated platform wins by an order of magnitude.
2. **The memory tax.** Hostwinds meters RAM at a flat **$1/GB/month** while used DDR4-2133 costs roughly **$2.80/GB** to buy — a 30-month payback on memory alone. Modern platforms with far higher DIMM capacity per socket turn that same metering into materially more revenue per rack unit. **The memory upsell, not the base server, is where the margin lives, and the legacy platform caps it.**
3. **The product gap.** The catalogue tops out at 3 TB HDD or 1 TB SATA SSD. **There is no NVMe anywhere, no GPU SKU has ever existed, and the newest CPU on offer is a 2017 Kaby Lake quad-core.** Meanwhile the group has just launched a ten-market colocation product and a "Quote Request" enterprise motion. **You cannot sell enterprise colocation and AI-adjacent capacity from a fleet whose flagship is a $200/month dual E5-2620 v3.** The competitive pressure is not from cheaper parts, it is from every rival selling NVMe and current-generation cores.
4. **Operational cost per box.** Self-assembly from mixed brokered stock produces a fleet with **no common firmware baseline, no vendor support path, no unified management plane**, and a hardware failure rate consistent with 9-to-19-year-old components. That is invisible in a BOM and enormous in an operating budget — and it is precisely the cost that consolidating three acquired estates into one facility exposes.

**Therefore the sales argument must be reframed from cost-per-server to revenue-per-kilowatt and revenue-per-rack-unit**, aimed at ColoCrossing's Buffalo expansion decision and funded from the $130M BMO facility — **not at Hostwinds' historical procurement habit, which no integrated platform will ever out-price.**

## 14. Supermicro sales angle

**Classification: greenfield AI — conditional, with purchasing authority possibly relocated.** This is **not** an incumbent-defence account (no OEM is confirmed), **not** a displacement account (no competing vendor is named), and **not** a platform-conversion account in the WebNX sense. It is new development, gated on two things.

**The honest wedge.** Hostwinds has **no GPU product at all**, a sellable fleet **roughly 8–19 years old** (newest part is a 2017 E3-1270 v6; **no Xeon Scalable, no EPYC anywhere**), and **1 Gbps customer ports throughout**. That is a large greenfield surface. But it is a **conditional** opportunity for two concrete reasons:

1. **Density.** Published colocation power caps at **3.84 kW per full 42U cabinet and 7.68 kW per 2-rack cage**. **H100/L40S-class deployments physically will not fit before a power and cooling upgrade.** Any AI proposal here has to be sold with a density solution attached, not as a server quote.
2. **Authority.** **Hostwinds was acquired by HostPapa on 2026-04-29, and ARIN re-registered AS54290 to HostPapa (Buffalo NY) on 2026-05-12.** Capex authority has very likely moved. **Confirm who holds purchasing before discussing a single specification.**

Also note the framing risk: because there is no GPU product, there is **no evidence of AI demand or AI capex intent** at this account either. Greenfield here means unproven, not merely uncontested.

**Do not open with "we know you're running Supermicro."** The research grade on hardware is **unverified**; the only signal is generic IPMI/BMC naming with zero iDRAC or iLO on the domain, and that is an inference. **Turn it into a question.**

**The qualifying question to ask on first contact — ask this one and nothing else:**

> **"After the acquisition, do server purchasing decisions for Seattle, Dallas and Amsterdam stay with Hostwinds, or have they moved to HostPapa?"**

Nothing else matters until that is answered. No quote, no assumed incumbent vendor, no specification discussion.

**Rule 8 distributor caution — read before anyone dials.** Hostwinds runs a sizeable fleet (303,360 IPv4 addresses, three data centers, ten colocation markets) yet **has never appeared in the CRM** — verified 2026-08-03 in salesleads Search (Type = All): no lead, no account, no do-not-call record. The reasonable inference is the same one that applies across this whole provider layer: **the servers are bought through distribution, not direct from the OEM.** Hostwinds' own "no middlemen" language refers to owning its hardware outright, and it names no supplier. **Under Rule 8 a distributor may cross territory boundaries, but it needs advance approval and a do-not-call flag.** Before the first call, confirm with the channel desk whether Hostwinds is already an existing account of a distributor. If it is, this shifts from new-account development to **co-selling on a distributor-held account**, and the opening, the quoting path and the registration method all change. **Sequence: confirm channel first, register the lead second, make contact last — reversing that order causes channel conflict.**

**Territory execution.** Tukwila is West Coast North = **T4 | T31**. **Team 1 is not eligible and cannot work this alone; neither T7 nor T11 covers this region** (T11 covers Chicago Area and Canada East and is not relevant here — do not route to it).
