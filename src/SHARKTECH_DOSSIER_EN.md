## 1. Bottom line

Sharktech, Inc. is a founder-owned, family-controlled DDoS-protected bare-metal, cloud and colocation provider headquartered in Las Vegas, Nevada, running its own ASN (AS46844) and leased cage/rack space across five sites — Las Vegas, Los Angeles, Denver, Chicago and Amsterdam — with roughly 25–50 staff and no outside investors. The commercial verdict is **displacement / second-source**, not incumbent defence: the one and only time Sharktech has ever named a server vendor in public, it named **Lenovo**, and "Supermicro" returns zero hits across every Sharktech-controlled property searched, so any opening that assumes existing Supermicro incumbency will fail on the first call. The single strongest piece of evidence in this file is Sharktech's own client-portal announcement #221 of 16 January 2025 launching the AMD EPYC 7702P line, whose spec block reads verbatim **"System: Lenovo"** ([announcement #221](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php/announcements/221/Now-Available-AMD-EPYC-7702P-with-NVMe-U.2.html)) — a first-party disclosure by the operator, not an inference. The one thing that could kill the deal is the channel: Sharktech buys through an **unnamed supplier/distributor** ("we received a new batch of EPYC 7702 servers from our supplier"), so under Rule 8 the distributor relationship must be cleared and a do-not-call flag confirmed *before* anyone dials — walking in without that risks a channel conflict on an account that has never once appeared in the CRM.

### 9.2 Cadence

**Basis — two independent evidence streams, reconciled.** **STREAM 1, catalogue archaeology:** 12 usable captures of [https://sharktech.net/dedicated-servers/](https://sharktech.net/dedicated-servers/) and [https://sharktech.net/servers.json](https://sharktech.net/servers.json) from 2022-12-07 to today, parsed at the SKU-row level. The 2026-07-28 and live captures were diffed at the `id` level from the servers.json feed. **STREAM 2, the company's own dated procurement announcements** at [portal.sharktech.net announcements](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php?rp=/announcements) (RSS, 27 items), which state purchases in plain language with dates. **Stream 2 consistently dates events EARLIER than Stream 1** (e.g. Gold 6148 announced in stock 2023-06-01 versus first catalogue sighting 2023-09-21), so Stream 2 is the leading indicator and Stream 1 is the confirmation. A [Web Hosting Talk offer](https://www.webhostingtalk.com/showthread.php?t=1898496) dated 2023-06-20 was used as a third-party cross-check on the 2023 pricing and spec.

**Observed intervals.** *Catalogue-observed new-model first-appearances (Stream 1):* Gold 6148 (≤2023-06-01) → E5-2695v4 (2024-08-06) = **~14.2 months**; E5-2695v4 → RTX A4000 (2024-12-01) = **3.9 months**; RTX A4000 → EPYC 7702P (2025-01-16 announced) = **1.5 months**; EPYC 7702P → Gold 6262V (2025-09-07) = **7.7 months**; Gold 6262V → Dual Gold 6248 (2026-07-01 announced) = **9.8 months**; Dual Gold 6248 → Dual EPYC 7702 (2026-07-13) = **0.4 months**; Dual EPYC 7702 → Gold 6248 fleet-wide (2026-07-21) = **0.3 months**; Gold 6248 → Gold 6246 (≤2026-08-10) = **0.6 months**. *Company-announced procurement events (Stream 2, stronger evidence):* 2023-06-01 → 2025-01-16 = **19.5 months**; 2025-01-16 → 2026-07-01 = **17.5 months**; 2026-07-01 → 2026-07-13 = **0.4 months**; 2026-07-13 → 2026-07-21 = **0.3 months**; 2026-07-21 → 2026-08-10 = **0.6 months**. **THE SHAPE IS UNMISTAKABLE: procurement is CAMPAIGN-BASED, not a steady cadence. The honest characterisation is: **long dormancy of 17–20 months, then a burst of 3–4 purchases inside 3–6 weeks. Plan against the campaign, not against the median.**

## 10. Cost ceiling

### 10.1 Rent-derived hardware cost ceiling

#### The assumption band — stated as ASSUMPTIONS, not findings

**EVERY OPERATING-COST FIGURE BELOW IS AN ASSUMPTION, NOT A RESEARCHED FACT.** These are industry norms for a DDoS-protected bare-metal host running 2U dual-socket nodes on owned/colocated racks. **None of them was sourced from Sharktech, from a filing, or from any vendor document.** They are stated so the arithmetic is auditable and so the reader can substitute their own numbers.

**ASSUMED monthly operating cost deducted from gross rent before computing the hardware cost ceiling:**

- **Power** (server draw plus cooling/PUE overhead): **ASSUMED** 400–800 W continuous for a 2U dual-socket node at an **ASSUMED** all-in $0.09–$0.16/kWh delivered → roughly **$40–$90 per node per month**.
- **Bandwidth / IP transit:** SKUs are sold at 10 Gbps with 300 TB/month allowance, but real blended utilisation is **ASSUMED** at 5–20% of the committed allowance → roughly **$15–$50 per node per month** at **ASSUMED** wholesale transit rates.
- **Rack space, cross-connects, facility:** **ASSUMED** **$20–$40 per node per month** amortised.
- **Remote hands, support, DDoS scrubbing capacity, management panel, billing, overhead:** **ASSUMED** **$15–$35 per node per month**.
- **TOTAL ASSUMED OPEX: roughly $90–$215 per node per month**, which for the SKU range below is equivalent to an opex ratio of **40% to 55% of gross rent**.

**MIDPOINT USED IN THE TABLE: a 50% opex ratio.** So monthly hardware contribution = `monthly_price × 0.50`, and:

```
ceiling_12mo = monthly_price × 0.50 × 12
ceiling_18mo = monthly_price × 0.50 × 18
ceiling_24mo = monthly_price × 0.50 × 24
```

At the **40% opex (optimistic)** end multiply every ceiling by **1.20**; at the **55% (pessimistic)** end multiply by **0.90**.

**FOUR FURTHER ASSUMPTIONS THAT MATERIALLY MOVE THE ANSWER, ALL UNVERIFIED:**

1. **100% occupancy is assumed.** Real bare-metal utilisation is rarely 100%. If a node sits idle 15% of its life, **every ceiling drops roughly 15%**. Sharktech's own announcements repeatedly say stock "usually sold out within 48 hours," which is a qualitative argument for high occupancy, but **it is marketing copy, not a utilisation figure**.
2. **List price is assumed collected.** Sharktech's published annual prepay is materially cheaper than 12× monthly — e.g. SKU 704 is $259/mo but **$2,641.80/yr, an effective $220.15/mo, about a 15% discount**. Any customer on annual prepay **cuts that SKU's ceiling by roughly 15%**.
3. **No hardware failure/replacement reserve is deducted.** On refurbished 2016–2019 silicon a replacement reserve is a real cost and would reduce ceilings further.
4. **Build labour is excluded.** [Announcement #243](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php?rp=/announcements/243) shows they hand-build ("we're currently building 12 of them"), so labour is a real per-node cost **not** in these figures.

**FACT, NOT ASSUMPTION, AND IT CONSTRAINS ALL OF THE ABOVE:** Sharktech raised Bare-Metal and Colocation prices by **9.25% effective 2025-04-01**, stating "as our own costs of operation continue to grow, we must keep up with the economy" and "It's been years since we increased our prices" ([announcement #223](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php?rp=/announcements/223)). Separately, they moved their entire Denver point of presence from H5 Data Centers to US Signal COO1 in May 2026 because H5 presented a "massive price increase" they refused to pass to clients ([announcement #235](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php?rp=/announcements/235)). **Both facts indicate the opex ratio is under upward pressure and that the 55% (pessimistic) end of the band is the safer planning assumption.**

#### What the gap means for pitching an integrated platform against self-assembly

1. **Self-assembly is not a preference here, it is the only arithmetic that works.** [Announcement #243](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php?rp=/announcements/243) (2026-07-01) says in their own words "we're upgrading our flagship Xeon Gold 6148 server to Dual Xeon Gold 6248 CPUs. We're currently building 12 of them in Las Vegas." **That is a CPU swap into an existing chassis, not a platform purchase.** Any pitch that assumes they buy complete new systems will be priced out before the second meeting.
2. **The squeeze is coming from memory and NAND, not from CPUs.** 2TB NVMe has moved from roughly $80–$100 in late 2025 to **$280–$450** now, and 32GB RDIMM sits near **$200** new. On SKU 704, **memory plus storage is $1,200 of a $1,529 BOM — 78%. CPU is only 18%.** The credible integrated-platform argument is therefore about **memory and storage sourcing leverage** and about **density** (more sellable nodes per rack per watt), **not** about processor pricing.
3. **Their pricing has almost no room left.** They took a **9.25% increase** on all Bare-Metal and Colocation on 2025-04-01 explicitly citing cost inflation, and in March 2026 moved the whole Denver footprint out of H5 rather than accept a facility price increase. **A platform pitch must lead with total cost per sellable node per month**, and should quantify the rack-space and power saving, because that is the lever they have already shown they will act on.
4. **Sell into the payback window they actually use.** On the evidence they underwrite somewhere between **12 and 18 months**. **Anything that cannot show payback inside 18 months at their published rents will not survive their internal maths.**
5. **The 40Gbps and 100Gbps network upgrade options across every SKU**, plus the completed **400GE transit upgrade (2026-06-25)**, mean onboard high-speed NICs carry real, quantifiable value to them — **that is $49-per-card of avoided cost today, but becomes a genuine differentiator at 40/100G.**

## 14. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **competitor account → displacement / second-source**

**Not** an incumbent-defence play and **not** a platform conversion of an unknown fleet. **The incumbent on the one platform they have ever named is Lenovo.** Sharktech buys through an unnamed reseller and integrates in-house in Las Vegas ("we're currently building 12 of them") — **which is precisely the customer shape a Supermicro barebone/BTO motion targets.**

**Three concrete wedges, in order of time-sensitivity:**

1. **The Denver migration window.** Sharktech moved its Denver point of presence from **H5 Data Centers to US Signal COO1**, starting 2026-05-01 and scheduled fully complete by 2026-05-31, announced personally by the CEO. Bare-metal customers got one-week move notices; colo customers had to clear equipment by 2026-05-29. **A physical DC migration is the classic hardware-refresh window** — and it is unresolved whether that fleet was relocated or replaced (see GAP below). ([Denver migration announcement](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php/announcements/236/Denver-migration.html))
2. **The GPU hole.** Their **only** GPU SKU is out of stock and two-to-three generations behind (RTX A4000 / V100; no SXM/HGX, no multi-GPU node, host CPU is 2016 Broadwell). They state openly that they "work with our vendors" to source GPU hardware on demand, and they have already proven they will buy an entire new platform family in a single batch — the Lenovo EPYC 7702P launch is the precedent. **A current-generation GPU platform is a greenfield product line for them.**
3. **Their own admitted 10G/40G/100G gap.** "if your server hardware already has a 10G capable network port (**most do**)" concedes that part of the installed base did not — and the 40G/100G upsell path they now advertise on all 51 SKUs is beyond what many Broadwell-era E5-2695v4 nodes can do.

**Wording discipline:** do not open by asserting Supermicro incumbency — the research grade is **GAP / explicit negative**. Do not assert the bulk-fleet chassis vendor — that grade is **inferred only**. **Ask, don't tell. Ask about the platform before quoting anything.**

### The qualifying question to ask on first contact

> **"Other than the Lenovo EPYC 7702P line, which supplier are the Xeon Gold 6248 and E5-2695v4 systems coming from? And after the Denver move completed, was that equipment refreshed or relocated as-is?"**

That single question resolves the two highest-value unknowns in this file at once — the identity of the channel incumbent behind ~50 of 51 SKUs, and whether a refresh budget has just been spent or is still ahead.

### Rule 8 distributor caution — read before dialling

Sharktech runs five sites, an ASN with **124,416 IPv4 addresses**, 51 orderable bare-metal SKUs and a claimed 1,000+ business customers across ~73 countries — **yet has never once appeared in the CRM** (verified 2026-08-03: no lead, no account, no do-not-call record). The only reasonable inference is that **their servers are bought through the distribution channel, not direct from the manufacturer.** Sharktech's own words corroborate it: "we received a new batch of EPYC 7702 servers from **our supplier**" — and that supplier is never named anywhere (see GAP).

**Under Rule 8, a distributor may cross territory boundaries, but it requires advance approval and a do-not-call flag.** Before anyone places the first call, confirm with the channel desk whether Sharktech is already an existing account of a distributor. If it is, this file converts from "new-business prospecting" to "distributor-co-managed existing account," and the opening, the quoting path and the registration method all change. **Sequence: clear the channel first → register the lead second → make contact last. Reversing that order creates a channel conflict.**
