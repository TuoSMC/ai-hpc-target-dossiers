## 1. Bottom line

Sharktech, Inc. is a founder-owned, family-controlled DDoS-protected bare-metal, cloud and colocation provider headquartered in Las Vegas, Nevada, running its own ASN (AS46844) and leased cage/rack space across five sites — Las Vegas, Los Angeles, Denver, Chicago and Amsterdam — with roughly 25–50 staff and no outside investors. The commercial verdict is **displacement / second-source**, not incumbent defence: the one and only time Sharktech has ever named a server vendor in public, it named **Lenovo**, and "Supermicro" returns zero hits across every Sharktech-controlled property searched, so any opening that assumes existing Supermicro incumbency will fail on the first call. The single strongest piece of evidence in this file is Sharktech's own client-portal announcement #221 of 16 January 2025 launching the AMD EPYC 7702P line, whose spec block reads verbatim **"System: Lenovo"** ([announcement #221](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php/announcements/221/Now-Available-AMD-EPYC-7702P-with-NVMe-U.2.html)) — a first-party disclosure by the operator, not an inference. The one thing that could kill the deal is the channel: Sharktech buys through an **unnamed supplier/distributor** ("we received a new batch of EPYC 7702 servers from our supplier"), so under Rule 8 the distributor relationship must be cleared and a do-not-call flag confirmed *before* anyone dials — walking in without that risks a channel conflict on an account that has never once appeared in the CRM.

## 11. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **competitor account → displacement / second-source**

**Not** an incumbent-defence play and **not** a platform conversion of an unknown fleet. **The incumbent on the one platform they have ever named is Lenovo.** Sharktech buys through an unnamed reseller and integrates in-house in Las Vegas ("we're currently building 12 of them") — **which is precisely the customer shape a Supermicro barebone/BTO motion targets.**

**Three concrete wedges, in order of time-sensitivity:**

1. **The Denver migration window.** Sharktech moved its Denver point of presence from **H5 Data Centers to US Signal COO1**, starting 2026-05-01 and scheduled fully complete by 2026-05-31, announced personally by the CEO. Bare-metal customers got one-week move notices; colo customers had to clear equipment by 2026-05-29. **A physical DC migration is the classic hardware-refresh window** — and it is unresolved whether that fleet was relocated or replaced (see GAP below). ([Denver migration announcement](https://portal.sharktech.net/index.php/announcements/236/Denver-migration.html))
2. **The GPU hole.** Their **only** GPU SKU is out of stock and two-to-three generations behind (RTX A4000 / V100; no SXM/HGX, no multi-GPU node, host CPU is 2016 Broadwell). They state openly that they "work with our vendors" to source GPU hardware on demand, and they have already proven they will buy an entire new platform family in a single batch — the Lenovo EPYC 7702P launch is the precedent. **A current-generation GPU platform is a greenfield product line for them.**
3. **Their own admitted 10G/40G/100G gap.** "if your server hardware already has a 10G capable network port (**most do**)" concedes that part of the installed base did not — and the 40G/100G upsell path they now advertise on all 51 SKUs is beyond what many Broadwell-era E5-2695v4 nodes can do.

**Wording discipline:** do not open by asserting Supermicro incumbency — the research grade is **GAP / explicit negative**. Do not assert the bulk-fleet chassis vendor — that grade is **inferred only**. **Ask, don't tell. Ask about the platform before quoting anything.**

### The qualifying question to ask on first contact

> **"Other than the Lenovo EPYC 7702P line, which supplier are the Xeon Gold 6248 and E5-2695v4 systems coming from? And after the Denver move completed, was that equipment refreshed or relocated as-is?"**

That single question resolves the two highest-value unknowns in this file at once — the identity of the channel incumbent behind ~50 of 51 SKUs, and whether a refresh budget has just been spent or is still ahead.

### Rule 8 distributor caution — read before dialling

Sharktech runs five sites, an ASN with **124,416 IPv4 addresses**, 51 orderable bare-metal SKUs and a claimed 1,000+ business customers across ~73 countries — **yet has never once appeared in the CRM** (verified 2026-08-03: no lead, no account, no do-not-call record). The only reasonable inference is that **their servers are bought through the distribution channel, not direct from the manufacturer.** Sharktech's own words corroborate it: "we received a new batch of EPYC 7702 servers from **our supplier**" — and that supplier is never named anywhere (see GAP).

**Under Rule 8, a distributor may cross territory boundaries, but it requires advance approval and a do-not-call flag.** Before anyone places the first call, confirm with the channel desk whether Sharktech is already an existing account of a distributor. If it is, this file converts from "new-business prospecting" to "distributor-co-managed existing account," and the opening, the quoting path and the registration method all change. **Sequence: clear the channel first → register the lead second → make contact last. Reversing that order creates a channel conflict.**
