## 3. Price to beat

### Assumptions — state these plainly, every time

Every "hardware ceiling" below answers one question: *what is the most this operator can pay for the hardware and still recover it inside N months of full occupancy?* The formula is:

> **ceiling = monthly rent × (1 − operating-cost deduction) × N months**

**The operating-cost deduction is an ASSUMPTION in every single case.** None of these six companies publishes cost or margin data; all are private and file no public accounts. The deductions used are industry-norm judgements, not researched facts:

| Operator | Deduction used | Band judged defensible | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|
| WebNX | **40%** | 30–50% | ±17% on every ceiling |
| Psychz | **45%** | 35–55% | ±18% |
| Sharktech | **50%** | 40–55% | +20% / −10% |
| ReliableSite | **per-component**, $42–$75/server/mo | $35–$140 | see below |
| Hostwinds / HostPapa | **50%** | 40–60% | ±20% |
| QuadraNet successor | **52.5%** | 45–60% | ±16% |

The only real-world anchor found anywhere is that **WebNX publishes its own retail colocation rate card** ([2025-01-24](https://www.webhostingtalk.com/showthread.php?t=1933969)): 1U/2U with ~400W, /29, 10G and 50TB at **$129/mo**; full rack 3KW/100G/100TB at **$1,500/mo**; 25-rack cage 75KW at **$23,000/mo**. That is a **retail** price to a third party and therefore an *upper bound* on internal housing cost, not internal cost itself. Psychz and Hostwinds publish comparable retail colo rates and the same caveat applies.

**Also excluded from every ceiling, all of which bias the numbers optimistically:** cost of capital (a 24-month payback at a realistic 10–12% is ~8–10% more demanding than shown); occupancy below 100%; residual value; assembly labour, burn-in, licensing, rails, freight, spares/RMA pool; and sales/support/G&A allocation. **Payback windows of 12/18/24 months are hurdle rates, not asset-life constraints** — dedicated-server hardware in this segment is routinely held 4–6 years.
