## 1. Bottom line

OpenMetal, Inc. is a separately-incorporated, separately-numbered operating company inside the InMotion Hosting group — not a product line and not an independent third party — headquartered at **2 Constitution Drive, Suite 101, Virginia Beach, VA 23462**, the same street address ARIN carries for InMotion Hosting, Inc. ([site footer](https://openmetal.io/contact-us/); [ARIN org INMOT-1](https://whois.arin.net/rest/org/INMOT-1.json)). It runs its own ARIN org (**OPENM-7**) and its own ASN (**AS400956**), but that ASN has exactly two BGP upstreams and **both are InMotion** — AS22611 and AS54641 — with no third-party transit and no PeeringDB registration at all ([RIPEstat](https://stat.ripe.net/data/asn-neighbours/data.json?resource=AS400956); [PeeringDB](https://www.peeringdb.com/api/net?asn=400956)). It sells OpenStack-based private cloud, bare metal and GPU servers out of four leased Tier III colocation sites — Ashburn VA (NTT VA1), Los Angeles (Digital Realty LAX10), Amsterdam and Singapore — and **discloses its own revenue on its own careers page: "$6M ARR to $18M ARR"** ([careers page](https://openmetal.io/about-openmetal/openmetal_careers/)). That is the single most valuable commercial datapoint in this file and it is first-party, not an aggregator estimate.

Commercially this is an **incumbent-defence account with a live, undetected displacement risk**. Supermicro is **confirmed** on two prior generations by two fully independent primary sources: OpenMetal's own live website still states that its XL v2.1 and XL v3 servers "are provisioned on Supermicro's SYS-120C-TN10R" ([XL v2.1](https://openmetal.io/resources/hardware-details/xl-v2-1-bare-metal-dedicated-server/), [XL v3](https://openmetal.io/resources/hardware-details/xl-v3-bare-metal-dedicated-server/), page metadata dateModified 2026-01-23), and **Supermicro's own published case study** (© 2021) names MBE-628E-822 MicroBlade enclosures and MBI-6219B-T41N blades powering InMotion's Flex Metal Cloud, OpenMetal's direct predecessor ([Success_Story_InMotion_V12.pdf](https://www.supermicro.com/CaseStudies/Success_Story_InMotion_V12.pdf)). **But from v4 onward they stopped naming anyone.** A direct negative test on the fetched raw HTML found **zero** occurrences of "Supermicro" and zero `SYS-` model strings on the XL v5 page, the H200 GPU page, the GPU pricing page and the complete hardware index — against four occurrences plus the model number on each of v2.1 and v3. Three readings are possible — a vendor switch, a marketing-copy change, or a mixed/ODM supply — and **public sources cannot distinguish between them. Do not assert Supermicro is the incumbent on the current generation.**

The timing is the strongest card in the file. The current GPU generation — **H200 NVL and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell** — went GA on **2026-06-17 into Ashburn, Virginia ONLY**, with "additional locations planned" and "Contact us about availability in other regions" in writing ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/openmetal-expands-v5-hardware-catalog-with-nvidia-rtx-pro-6000-blackwell-and-h200-private-gpu-servers-302803270.html); [GPU pricing page](https://openmetal.io/gpu-servers-clusters-pricing/)). The **previous** GPU generation (A100/H100, GA 2025-04-11) shipped into **both U.S. East and U.S. West on day one** ([2025 press release](https://openmetal.io/resources/media-and-press/press-release-openmetal-unveils-private-gpu-servers-for-ai-and-ml/)). So West Coast GPU demand is already proven, and LAX10, Amsterdam and Singapore are three built-out sites carrying **zero** current-generation GPU. That geographic catch-up — not a generational refresh — is the near-term purchase, and it plausibly lands **Q4 2026 – Q2 2027**.

Three things could kill this. **First, the channel:** an account with a published Supermicro case study is almost certainly served through a distributor, and the 2021 MicroBlade footprint implies an incumbent channel partner with the relationship. Under Rule 8 the distributor position must be cleared before the first call. **Second, the name you search:** the CRM was verified clean for "OpenMetal", but the 2021 case study was published under **InMotion Hosting**, and the two entities share a street address, a NOC phone block and a BGP path — searching only one name and concluding the account is unclaimed would be the single most expensive mistake available here. **Third, the territory.** The brief places this in Los Angeles / T1; the evidence does not support that. Los Angeles is a leased Digital Realty cage with no current-generation GPU in it. The HQ, the CTO, the CFO, the CLO, the President, the GPU fleet and the NOC are all on the East Coast. **Re-run the territory test on the Virginia Beach HQ before registering** — see §13.

2. **The levers that DO move it are the ones tied to their specific published constraints:** DIMM slot count and **1DPC DDR5-6400** support, because they buy half-populated at **16 of 32 slots** specifically to preserve upgrade headroom; **NVMe bay count**, because they specify up to 8 bays with boot/data isolation; **TDX and SGX enablement out of the box with no reconfiguration**, which they market as a differentiator; **full IPMI and BIOS access** for PXE-booting immutable OS images; and above all **thermal and power efficiency per rack**, because **NTT VA1 caps them at 22kW per rack** and that ceiling — not the purchase price — is what limits how much revenue they can put in a cage.

## 13. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **incumbent defence with a live displacement risk — not greenfield, and not a straightforward displacement either**

The evidence says this is an **existing Supermicro account that may already have been lost on the current generation, and nobody appears to have noticed.** Supermicro published a case study on this customer group in 2021 (MicroBlade MBE-628E-822 and MBI-6219B-T41N powering InMotion's Flex Metal Cloud, the direct predecessor of OpenMetal), there was a joint press release, and **OpenMetal's own website still says today** that its XL v2.1 and XL v3 servers "are provisioned on Supermicro's SYS-120C-TN10R."

**But the current generation was tested directly, and the word "Supermicro" appears ZERO times** in the raw HTML of the XL v5 page, the H200 GPU page, the GPU pricing page and the full hardware index — while appearing **four times each** on v2.1 and v3. **They named us for two generations and stopped naming anyone from v4 onward. That is the whole opportunity and the whole risk in one fact.**

### FIRST ACTION IS INTERNAL, NOT EXTERNAL

**Before any outreach, check whether an existing Supermicro account team, prior deal history, or an existing registration covers InMotion Hosting, Inc. or OpenMetal, Inc.** The CRM was verified clean for "OpenMetal" (2026-08-11) — **but the 2021 case study was published under INMOTION HOSTING**, and the two entities share a street address, a NOC phone block and a BGP path. **A clean search on one name proves nothing about the other. Searching only "OpenMetal" and concluding the account is unclaimed would be the single most expensive mistake available here.**

### Rule 8 — distributor caution (read before dialling)

An account with a **published Supermicro case study** is almost certainly being served through a distributor or reseller, and the 2021 MicroBlade footprint means there is likely an **incumbent channel partner with the relationship**. **Do not go direct, do not quote around the channel, and do not register this until the distributor position is established.** If a partner already owns the account, the correct move is to bring them the v5/GPU refresh intelligence and **co-sell** — the value added by this dossier is the timing and the technical wedge, **not the right to transact**.

Note also that the group's stated partner list **allegedly** includes Dell alongside Supermicro. That is **unverified** (§5.1) — but if true, there is an **incumbent competitor already inside the tent**, and it should be verified rather than ignored.

### The one qualifying question for first contact

Ask this of **Jamie Tischart (CTO)**, and ask it **exactly once, early**:

> **"Your site still says the XL v2.1 and v3 were provisioned on Supermicro SYS-120C-TN10R, but the v4, v5 and the new H200 and RP6000 pages don't name a platform vendor at all. What changed at v4, and who is building the GPU nodes today?"**

That single question resolves the incumbency status, surfaces the competitor if there is one, tells you whether this is **defence or displacement** — and does it **without revealing that you do not already know**.

### The four levers that actually matter to this buyer, in order

1. **POWER DENSITY, NOT PRICE PER BOX.** Their only GPU site, **NTT VA1 in Ashburn, is capped at 22kW per rack**. At roughly 2–2.5kW for a dual-H200-NVL node they get about **8–10 nodes per rack**. They are a colocation **TENANT** at Digital Realty and NTT with **no owned-facility advantage**, so every additional revenue-generating node per rack is **pure margin** to them. **Lead with nodes-per-rack inside a 22kW envelope, not with unit price** — and note their own top case study is literally titled "How a Public Cloud Provider Increased Density, Modernized Hardware, and Slashed Costs", so **density is a story they already sell downstream.**
2. **THE ACCELERATOR IS 70–80% OF THE BOM, SO CHASSIS PRICE IS NOT THE BATTLEGROUND.** Two H200 NVL cards at $28k–35k street dominate a roughly $73k–99k node. Competing on host cost fights over **$17k–29k**. Compete instead on their **published technical non-negotiables**: 32 DIMM slots at 1DPC DDR5-6400 (they deliberately populate only 16 of 32 to preserve headroom), up to 8 NVMe bays with boot/data isolation, **TDX and SGX enabled out of the box with no reconfiguration**, full IPMI and BIOS access for PXE-booting immutable images, and their unusual published interest in **an attested GPU inside an Intel TDX trust domain**.
3. **ONE PLATFORM WIN CAPTURES THE WHOLE CATALOGUE.** Medium v5, Large v5, XL v5, the H200 SKU and the RP6000 SKU **all run the same dual-socket Xeon 6 host**, differentiated only by CPU SKU, DIMM population, drive count and accelerator. **This is not a SKU sale, it is a platform standardisation sale — and correspondingly, losing the platform loses everything at once.**
4. **TIMING IS THE STRONGEST CARD.** The current GPU generation went GA on **2026-06-17 into Ashburn ONLY**, with "additional locations planned" and "Contact us about availability in other regions" **in writing**. The previous generation shipped to **US East AND US West on day one**, so West Coast GPU demand is already proven. **Los Angeles LAX10, Amsterdam and Singapore are three built-out sites with zero current-generation GPU.** That expansion — not a generational refresh — is the near-term purchase, and it plausibly lands **Q4 2026 – Q2 2027**.

### Right-size the pursuit, and do not over-invest

They self-disclose **$6M ARR** on their own careers page, targeting **$18M**. Their bare metal FAQ says they keep **"up to 12 of a particular type in a particular data center"** on hand and that initial customer deployments of **"3 and 6 are common"**. This is a buyer who transacts in units of roughly **3–12 nodes on a repeating cadence with long price-lock tails**, not a hundred-node tender. **It will not clear a $100M threshold and should not be resourced as though it might.**

Its real value is **annuity and reference**: a fast-follower on Intel silicon (Granite Rapids within about 15 months), a visible **OpenInfra Foundation member and OpenStack Foundation corporate sponsor**, and a company **whose customers are themselves cloud and hosting providers** — so the logo compounds.

### Use their own words on cost — and do not overreach

They published on **2026-05-15** that server DRAM rose nearly **95%** in early 2026, that "this affects every infrastructure provider including OpenMetal", that "OpenMetal's new v5 hardware reflects current market pricing", and pointedly that **"anyone telling you they've found a way around the supply crunch is worth scrutinizing carefully."**

**That last line is a warning. Do not walk in claiming to have beaten the supply crunch — they will see straight through it, and they have said so in public.** Lead instead with **supply certainty**, **delivery-date reliability** against their own promise that servers are "delivered on schedule or your first month free", and **density economics** — the three things a margin-squeezed colo tenant on a fixed-price rental model actually needs.
