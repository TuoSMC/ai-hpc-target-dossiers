## 1. Bottom line

OpenMetal, Inc. is a separately-incorporated OpenStack private-cloud, bare-metal and GPU operator sitting inside the InMotion Hosting group, headquartered at **2 Constitution Drive, Suite 101, Virginia Beach, VA 23462** ([site footer](https://openmetal.io/contact-us/), carrying "2022-2026 © OpenMetal, Inc."; the identical address is ARIN-registered to InMotion Hosting, Inc. under org [INMOT-1](https://whois.arin.net/rest/org/INMOT-1.json), registered 2008-06-03). It self-declares **$6M ARR, targeting $18M**, on its own live careers page ([OpenMetal careers](https://openmetal.io/about-openmetal/openmetal_careers/)) — the single most valuable and best-sourced commercial datapoint in this file, because it is a first-party disclosure rather than an aggregator estimate.

**This is an existing Supermicro account that may already have been lost on the current generation, and the loss appears to have gone unnoticed.** OpenMetal's own live website still states that its **XL v2.1 and XL v3 bare metal servers "are provisioned on Supermicro's SYS-120C-TN10R"** ([XL v2.1](https://openmetal.io/resources/hardware-details/xl-v2-1-bare-metal-dedicated-server/), [XL v3](https://openmetal.io/resources/hardware-details/xl-v3-bare-metal-dedicated-server/); page metadata dateModified 2026-01-23, so the claim is still being maintained), and Supermicro's own published case study (© 2021) names **MBE-628E-822 MicroBlade enclosures with MBI-6219B-T41N blade nodes** powering InMotion's Flex Metal Cloud, the direct predecessor of OpenMetal ([Supermicro case study PDF](https://www.supermicro.com/CaseStudies/Success_Story_InMotion_V12.pdf)). But a direct negative test against the raw HTML of the [XL v5 page](https://openmetal.io/resources/hardware-details/xl-v5-bare-metal-dedicated-server/), the [H200 GPU page](https://openmetal.io/resources/hardware-details/gpu-server-h200/), the [GPU pricing page](https://openmetal.io/gpu-servers-clusters-pricing/) and the [complete hardware index](https://openmetal.io/openmetal-hardware-index/) returns **zero occurrences of "Supermicro" and zero "SYS-" model strings**, against four occurrences plus the model number on each of v2.1 and v3. **They named us for two generations and have named nobody since v4.** That single asymmetry is simultaneously the opportunity, the risk, and the first question to ask.

Two things must be cleared before anyone dials. **First, the channel.** A customer with a published Supermicro case study is almost certainly served through a distributor or reseller, and the 2021 MicroBlade footprint implies an incumbent channel partner. The CRM was verified clean for "OpenMetal" — but the 2021 case study was published under **InMotion Hosting**, and the two entities share a street address, a NOC phone block and a BGP path. A clean search on one name proves nothing about the other. **Second, the territory.** The brief places this account in Los Angeles / T1; the evidence does not support that. Los Angeles is a leased Digital Realty LAX10 cage with **no current-generation GPU in it**; the corporate decision centre, the CTO, the CFO, the CLO, the President, the GPU fleet and the network all sit in Virginia. See the territory warning in §2 and §14 — escalate before registering.

Commercially, right-size it. This is a repeating **3-to-12-node buyer** with long price-lock tails ([bare metal pricing FAQ](https://openmetal.io/bare-metal-pricing/): "we typically can deploy up to 12 of a particular type in a particular data center at any given time"; initial deployments "can be of any size but 3 and 6 are common"), **not** a hundred-node tender, and nothing in the public record supports a $100M+ characterisation. Its value is annuity plus reference: one host platform spans Medium v5, Large v5, XL v5 and both GPU SKUs, so a single platform standardisation win captures the whole catalogue — and losing the platform loses all of it at once.

- **The corporate decision centre is Virginia Beach, VA.** HQ address, every press-release dateline, and the President, CTO, CFO and CLO all sit East Coast. The network terminates in InMotion's Virginia NOC.
- **The genuine California nexus is narrow but real:** (a) a second, older ARIN org "InMotion Hosting" (handle [INMOT](https://whois.arin.net/rest/org/INMOT.json)) registered **2004-06-14** at 2800 28th St., Suite 321, **Santa Monica, CA 90405**; (b) **Sash Ghosh**, Head of Growth and Partnerships, is Santa Monica, CA per her own [FEC filings](https://api.open.fec.gov/v1/schedules/schedule_a/?contributor_employer=OpenMetal) and carries a (323) LA-area phone on OpenMetal press releases; (c) InMotion Hosting's first data center was in Los Angeles in 2001 ([Todd Robinson profile](https://openmetal.io/about-openmetal/team-page/team-profile-todd-robinson/)).
- **Action:** re-run the territory test against the **Virginia Beach HQ** before registering. This reads as a T2/East-Coast-owned account with a California satellite, and a T1 registration may be challenged.

## 4. Leadership & ownership

Evidence grades used in this section: **primary-record** = internet-number registry, corporate registry, campaign-finance filing, or the company's own published page · **corroborated** = two or more independent sources agree · **single-source** = one source only, no corroboration · **GAP** = nothing found after a named search.

**Four framing facts before the table.**

**First — the corporate relationship is now settled, and it decides who signs.** OpenMetal, Inc. is **not** the same legal entity as InMotion Hosting, Inc. and **not** merely a brand. ## 14. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **Incumbent defence with a live displacement risk**

**This is neither greenfield nor a straightforward displacement.** The evidence says this is an **EXISTING Supermicro account that may already have been lost on the current generation, and nobody appears to have noticed.**

Supermicro published a case study on this customer group in **2021** (MicroBlade **MBE-628E-822** and **MBI-6219B-T41N** powering InMotion's Flex Metal Cloud, the direct predecessor of OpenMetal), there was a **joint press release**, and **OpenMetal's own website still says today** that its XL v2.1 and XL v3 servers "are provisioned on Supermicro's **SYS-120C-TN10R**." But the current generation was tested directly and **the word "Supermicro" appears ZERO times in the raw HTML of the XL v5 page, the H200 GPU page, the GPU pricing page and the full hardware index — while appearing four times each on v2.1 and v3.** They named us for two generations and stopped naming anyone from v4 onward. **That is the whole opportunity and the whole risk in one fact.**

### FIRST ACTION IS INTERNAL, NOT EXTERNAL

**Before any outreach, check whether an existing account team, prior deal history, or an existing registration covers InMotion Hosting, Inc. or OpenMetal, Inc.** The CRM was checked clean for "OpenMetal" — **but the 2021 case study was published under INMOTION HOSTING**, and the two entities share a street address, a NOC phone block and a BGP path. **A clean search on one name proves nothing about the other. Searching only "OpenMetal" and concluding the account is unclaimed would be the single most expensive mistake available here.**

### Rule 8 — distributor caution (read before dialling)

**An account with a published Supermicro case study is almost certainly being served through a distributor or reseller**, and the 2021 MicroBlade footprint means there is likely an **incumbent channel partner with the relationship**. **Do not go direct, do not quote around the channel, and do not register this until the distributor position is established.** If a partner already owns the account, the correct move is to bring them the v5/GPU refresh intelligence and **co-sell** — the value added here is the **timing and the technical wedge, not the right to transact.**

Also note that the group's stated partner list **allegedly** includes Dell alongside Supermicro. **That is unverified** (§6.1) — **but if true there is an incumbent competitor already inside the tent.**

### The one qualifying question for first contact

Ask this of **Jamie Tischart (CTO)**, and ask it exactly once, early:

> **"Your site still says the XL v2.1 and v3 were provisioned on Supermicro SYS-120C-TN10R, but the v4, v5 and the new H200 and RP6000 pages don't name a platform vendor at all. What changed at v4, and who is building the GPU nodes today?"**

That single question **resolves the incumbency status, surfaces the competitor if there is one, tells you whether this is defence or displacement — and does it without revealing that you do not already know.**

### The four levers that actually matter to this buyer, in order

1. **POWER DENSITY, NOT PRICE PER BOX.** Their only GPU site, **NTT VA1 in Ashburn, is capped at 22 kW per rack.** At roughly 2–2.5 kW for a dual-H200-NVL node they get about **8–10 nodes per rack.** They are a colocation **TENANT** at Digital Realty and NTT with **no owned-facility advantage**, so **every additional revenue-generating node per rack is pure margin to them.** Lead with **nodes-per-rack inside a 22 kW envelope**, not unit price — and note their own top case study is literally titled *"How a Public Cloud Provider Increased Density, Modernized Hardware, and Slashed Costs."* **Density is a story they already sell downstream.**
2. **THE ACCELERATOR IS 70–80% OF THE BOM, SO CHASSIS PRICE IS NOT THE BATTLEGROUND.** Two H200 NVL cards at **$28k–$35k street** dominate a roughly **$73k–$99k node**. Competing on host cost fights over **$17k–$29k**. Compete instead on their **published technical non-negotiables**: 32 DIMM slots at 1DPC DDR5-6400 (they deliberately populate only **16 of 32** to preserve headroom); **up to 8 NVMe bays with boot/data isolation**; **TDX and SGX enabled out of the box with no reconfiguration**; **full IPMI and BIOS access** for PXE-booting immutable images like Talos and Flatcar; and their unusual published interest in **an attested GPU inside an Intel TDX trust domain**.
3. **ONE PLATFORM WIN CAPTURES THE WHOLE CATALOGUE.** Medium v5, Large v5, XL v5, the H200 SKU and the RP6000 SKU **all run the same dual-socket Xeon 6 host**, differentiated only by CPU SKU, DIMM population, drive count and accelerator. **This is not a SKU sale, it is a platform standardisation sale — and correspondingly, losing the platform loses everything at once.**
4. **TIMING IS THE STRONGEST CARD.** The current GPU generation went GA on **2026-06-17 into Ashburn ONLY**, with "**additional locations planned**" and "Contact us about availability in other regions" **in writing**.
