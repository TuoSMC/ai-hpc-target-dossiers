## 1. Bottom line

Hot Aisle Inc. is a two-person, AMD-exclusive bare-metal GPU cloud that runs a single production cluster — roughly **16 × Dell PowerEdge XE9680 chassis carrying ~128 × AMD Instinct MI300X** — as a colocation tenant inside Switch's "The Pyramid" campus in Grand Rapids, Michigan ([hotaisle.xyz/mi300x](https://hotaisle.xyz/mi300x), [/datacenter](https://hotaisle.xyz/datacenter), read 2026-08-11). Commercially this is a **greenfield displacement on the next generation, not an incumbent-defence account and not a platform conversion** — there is no evidence Hot Aisle has ever bought, evaluated or publicly mentioned Supermicro, and equally no prior loss to overcome. Dell owns the installed base and owns it deservedly: Hot Aisle publicly praised Dell for dispatching two technicians to fix a single loose-screw GPU and called the support "breathtaking" ([Cruising to the finish line, 2024-09-13](https://hotaisle.xyz/blog/cruising-to-the-finish-line/)). **Do not try to win that fleet.**

What is genuinely open is the **next** cluster. Hot Aisle raised its MI300X price from $1.99 to $2.99/GPU/hr on 2026-07-14 because it is "at 100% capacity" with "a queue of customers waiting" ([Why We Raised Our MI300X Price](https://hotaisle.xyz/blog/why-we-raised-our-mi300x-price)); it states **">$50 million in customer requests for MI355X capacity"** and that **"The constraint is access to hardware, not demand for the platform"**; and it is raising **$50–100M across "equity, strategic investment, and asset finance"** explicitly "to purchase AMD MI355X systems and fund the networking, rack integration, and site deployment" ([Investors page](https://hotaisle.xyz/investors)). Its own MI355X page still says verbatim **"We are still raising the capital required to buy and deploy the hardware"** ([/mi355x](https://hotaisle.xyz/mi355x)) — so the platform is **not** locked, even though Advizex's case study says the MI355X cluster will be built "working with Advizex every step of the way" ([Advizex case study](https://www.advizex.com/case-studies/advizex-and-hot-aisle)). **Integrator commitment is not chassis commitment.**

The single strongest technical wedge is cooling. Hot Aisle's own MI355X page specifies **1,400 W per accelerator in a rack design using direct liquid cooling** — but they are a *tenant* at Switch and do not control the cooling plant. Supermicro ships an **air-cooled 10U 8× MI355X system** alongside liquid-cooled variants ([Supermicro press release](https://www.supermicro.com/en/pressreleases/supermicro-expands-its-portfolio-performance-and-efficiency-driven-air-cooled-ai)), which removes a landlord dependency from the critical path of a capital-constrained company whose only stated blocker is hardware access. That is a capability argument, not a discount argument.

Two things could kill this deal. First, **channel**: Advizex is a Dell Titanium Solution Provider that ran four months of weekly planning calls, staged the hardware in its own office space and is already named as the partner for the next build — a bare-chassis quote loses to that on completeness regardless of unit price, so the authorized distributor/integrator must be selected and registered **before** first substantive contact under Rule 8. Second, **price**: the publicly listed Supermicro 8× MI300X platform at **$275,863.87** ([Supermicro eStore](https://store.supermicro.com/us_en/8u-gpu-superserver-as-8125gs-tnmr2.html)) is **$34,483 per GPU**, which clears only a 24-month payback at the lean end of the modelled operating-cost band and does not clear 18 months at all (§9). List price does not close this account; **financing structure and lead time** do — and their own naming of "asset finance" is the invitation to bring both.
