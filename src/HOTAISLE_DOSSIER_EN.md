## 1. Bottom line

Hot Aisle Inc. is a **two-person, AMD-exclusive bare-metal GPU cloud** running roughly **16 Dell PowerEdge XE9680 chassis / ~128 AMD Instinct MI300X** as a colocation tenant inside Switch's "The Pyramid" campus in Grand Rapids / Caledonia, Michigan, on **AS21566**, with no office anywhere and a Cheyenne, Wyoming mail drop as its address of record ([hotaisle.xyz/datacenter](https://hotaisle.xyz/datacenter); [ARIN Org HA-716](https://rdap.arin.net/registry/entity/HA-716), read 2026-08-11). Commercially this is a **greenfield displacement on the NEXT generation — not incumbent defence, and emphatically not a rip-and-replace pitch.** Dell owns the installed base and owns it deservedly: Hot Aisle publicly called Dell's support "breathtaking" after Dell sent two technicians to fix a single loose-screw GPU ([Hot Aisle blog, 2024-09-13](https://hotaisle.xyz/blog/cruising-to-the-finish-line/)). You will not win that fleet and should not try.

What is genuinely open is the **MI355X build, which is unpurchased and uncapitalised**. Every precondition for a purchase is already public and stated by the company itself: they are **at 100% capacity with a customer queue**, they **raised the MI300X price from $1.99 to $2.99/GPU/hr on 2026-07-14** because of it, they claim **">$50 million in customer requests for MI355X capacity"**, and they are **raising $50–100M "across equity, strategic investment, and asset finance"** explicitly "to purchase AMD MI355X systems and fund the networking, rack integration, and site deployment" ([pricing](https://hotaisle.xyz/pricing); [Why We Raised Our MI300X Price](https://hotaisle.xyz/blog/why-we-raised-our-mi300x-price); [investors](https://hotaisle.xyz/investors)). Their own words: **"The constraint is access to hardware, not demand for the platform."**

Two things make this winnable rather than wishful. First, **they skip generations when capital is short** — MI325X was teased in November 2024 and never deployed, a negative finding established by an empty Wayback CDX result set for `hotaisle.xyz/mi325x` across the entire archive — so the MI355X platform decision is genuinely uncommitted rather than a refresh of an existing estate. Second, **their own MI355X page specifies 1,400 W per accelerator in a rack design using direct liquid cooling**, and they are a *tenant* who does not control the cooling plant ([hotaisle.xyz/mi355x](https://hotaisle.xyz/mi355x)). Supermicro's **air-cooled 10U 8× MI355X** platform removes a landlord dependency from the critical path of a capital-constrained company whose only stated blocker is hardware access. That is a capability argument, not a discount argument, and it is the strongest thing in this file.

Three things can kill it. **(1) Advizex** — the Dell Titanium integrator that ran four months of weekly planning calls, staged the hardware in its own office and is already named in writing as the partner for the next cluster ([Advizex case study](https://www.advizex.com/case-studies/advizex-and-hot-aisle)); displace the chassis, never the integrator. **(2) List price** — at $275,863.87 for the MI300X-generation Supermicro equivalent, the box only clears a 24-month payback against their own published rents, and comes nowhere near 12 or 18 months (§10). **(3) Channel and territory** — the datacenter is Michigan (T1) but the entity of record is Wyoming and the two principals appear to sit in New Hampshire and Washington State, so the T1 claim must be documented on the Michigan production footprint at the moment of registration, and the distributor position must be cleared under Rule 8 before pricing is discussed (§14).

## 14. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **GREENFIELD DISPLACEMENT ON THE NEXT GENERATION**

**Not incumbent defence — we have no incumbency. Not platform conversion — do not ask them to rip out a working 128-GPU MI300X cluster.**

The honest read: **Dell owns the installed base and owns it deservedly.** Hot Aisle publicly praised Dell for dispatching two technicians to fix a single loose-screw GPU and called the support "breathtaking" ([2024-09-13](https://hotaisle.xyz/blog/cruising-to-the-finish-line/)). You will not win that fleet and should not try. **What is genuinely open is the NEXT cluster** — the MI355X build, which is unpurchased, uncapitalised and, critically, **a generation they have never deployed**, so there is no MI325X estate creating standardisation gravity. They already skipped one generation for capital reasons, which proves the platform decision reopens each cycle.

### The five facts that make this winnable

1. **They are at 100% capacity with a customer queue and just raised prices from $1.99 to $2.99 because of it** — demand is not the constraint ([pricing](https://hotaisle.xyz/pricing); [price post](https://hotaisle.xyz/blog/why-we-raised-our-mi300x-price)).
2. **They state ">$50 million in customer requests for MI355X capacity"** and that "**The constraint is access to hardware, not demand for the platform**" ([investors](https://hotaisle.xyz/investors)).
3. **They are raising $50–100M explicitly "to purchase AMD MI355X systems and fund the networking, rack integration, and site deployment," and they name ASSET FINANCE as one of three routes** — financing is an open lever, not a settled constraint.
4. **Their MI355X page says outright "We are still raising the capital required to buy and deploy the hardware"** — the platform has not been locked, even though Advizex's case study says they will build the MI355X cluster with Advizex. **Integrator commitment ≠ chassis commitment.**
5. **Their automation is vendor-agnostic by construction** — SONiC, PXE, cloud-init, NUMA-balanced KVM, NetBox as source of truth — so the switching cost is **BMC/Redfish endpoints and NetBox templates, not a rewrite. Say that out loud to Clint Armstrong; it is the objection he will raise and the one we can actually answer.**

### The technical wedge — stronger than any discount

Their own MI355X page specifies **1,400 W per accelerator** in "a deployable rack design" using **direct liquid cooling**. **They are a colocation TENANT at Switch Pyramid — they do not control the cooling plant.** If DLC is unavailable, capacity-limited, or slow to provision in their leased footprint, **their entire MI355X plan has a facility dependency on its critical path.**

Supermicro ships an **air-cooled 10U 8× MI355X system** (2.3 TB HBM3E, 5th Gen EPYC, up to 72 cores) alongside liquid-cooled variants ([press release](https://www.supermicro.com/en/pressreleases/supermicro-expands-its-portfolio-performance-and-efficiency-driven-air-cooled-ai)). **Removing a landlord dependency from the critical path of a capital-constrained company that has publicly said hardware access is its only blocker is a capability argument, not a price argument — and it is the single best reason for them to take the meeting.**

### Second wedge — lead time is a price for this buyer

Supermicro's 8× MI300X platform shows **in stock, ships 3-5 business days** ([eStore](https://store.supermicro.com/us_en/8u-gpu-superserver-as-8125gs-tnmr2.html)); the Dell XE9680 MI300X path is **quote-only with roughly four-week quoted delivery** at reseller level, **on top of the four months of planning calls their first build required**. For a company whose revenue is capped by physical capacity and whose customers are queued, weeks of lead time convert directly into lost revenue. **Quantify it using their own published prices: every idle week of an 8-GPU node at $3.39/GPU/hr is about $4,550 of unbilled capacity.**

### Third wedge — repeatable unit economics

Their stated expansion model is "smaller inference-focused sites," "compact, repeatable inference deployments," with "existing automation" that brings each site online "without rebuilding the process at every site" ([investors](https://hotaisle.xyz/investors)). **They are not buying one cluster; they are buying a TEMPLATE they intend to stamp out regionally. Win the reference design once and you win the standard. Price and structure the first node accordingly.**
