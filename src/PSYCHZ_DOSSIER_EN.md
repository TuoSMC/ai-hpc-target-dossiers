## 1. Bottom line

Psychz Networks is the operating brand of **Profuse Solutions, Inc.**, a founder-controlled California corporation that runs a self-operated ~15,000 sq ft Los Angeles datacenter at 700 Wilshire Blvd plus leased space across 16–17 interconnection facilities worldwide, selling bare-metal dedicated servers, colocation, DDoS mitigation, IP transit, CDN and — newly — quote-only NVIDIA GPU servers on **AS40676**. The commercial verdict is **incumbent defence + refresh + GPU line extension**: Supermicro is a strong circumstantial read on the existing fleet, their own reference architecture shows a live 3rd/4th/5th Gen Xeon Scalable refresh cycle underway, and they are marketing H100/H200/B200/B300 capacity with **no configurator, no published price and no named platform vendor** — the signature of a provider sourcing GPU nodes deal-by-deal rather than off a standing supply agreement. The single strongest piece of evidence is that Psychz **authors and hosts Supermicro-specific BMC documentation on its own support domain** ([Access Supermicro IPMI Remote Console](https://www.psychz.net/client/question/en/access-supermicro-ipmi-remote-console.html), plus a Supermicro IPMI default-password article), while a site-restricted search of psychz.net returns **no Dell iDRAC and no HPE iLO equivalents** — and their live inventory carries a **Core i7-7700K server SKU that Dell and HPE do not manufacture**. The one thing that could kill the deal is the **channel**: this is a sizeable multi-site fleet that has never once appeared in the CRM, which points at purchase through distribution — under Rule 8 a distributor may cross territory boundaries but needs advance approval and a do-not-call flag, so calling before the channel is checked, or opening with "we know you run Supermicro" (which is inference, not disclosure), burns credibility on the first call.

## 10. Cost ceiling

### 10.1 Rent-derived ceiling

**Method.** Ceiling = **monthly price × contribution margin × payback months**, where contribution margin is the share of gross rental revenue left after operating cost. **All ceilings below are computed at a 55% contribution margin (a 45% operating-cost deduction)**, the midpoint of the band stated next. Ceilings are rounded to the nearest dollar.

**READ THIS BEFORE THE TABLE — the operating-cost deduction is an ASSUMPTION, not a researched fact.** This is the single most important caveat in this section. Psychz publishes **no cost data, no margin data and no financials** — the entity is private and files no public accounts. The band below is an **industry-norm judgement applied by the analyst**, and every ceiling figure moves proportionally with it. **At a 35% deduction the ceilings rise by ~18%; at a 55% deduction they fall by ~18%. Treat the ceilings as an order-of-magnitude envelope, not a computed fact.**

- **ASSUMPTION — operating cost = 35% to 55% of gross monthly rental revenue, midpoint 45%**, covering power, bandwidth, rack/floor, DDoS mitigation, IPv4, remote hands, provisioning labour, support, and an allowance for churn and rack vacancy. The **low end (35%)** reflects their **owned 15,000 sq ft Los Angeles floor**, where there is no third-party colocation margin to pay; the **high end (55%)** reflects partner facilities such as Ashburn, Dallas, London, Singapore and Tokyo where they are a **colocation tenant paying someone else's margin**. Note that the LA-versus-Ashburn price spread on identical E5-1650 v3 silicon (**$462 versus $164**) runs in the **opposite** direction to a pure cost story and is more likely demand/positioning-driven — **so do not read the owned floor as automatically the cheaper site.**
- **SOURCED ANCHORS that partially constrain the assumption — these ARE researched facts**, from the company's own published Dallas colocation price list (plan 224): rack space **47U at $105/mo**, implying **$2.23 per U per month**. First power circuit priced at **1.92 kW / 20A / 120V = $274/mo**; **2.88 kW / 30A / 120V = $399/mo**; **3.33 kW / 20A / 208V = $469/mo**; **4.99 kW / 30A / 208V = $724/mo** — the top tier implying roughly **$145 per kW per month at retail**. Bandwidth: **30 TB included**, 60 TB **+$39/mo**, 100 TB **+$89/mo**, 1 Gbps unmetered **+$225/mo**, 10 Gbps unmetered **+$199/mo**. Source: [plan 224 JSON](https://www.psychz.net/dashboard/client/web/catalog/plan/224.json).
- **HOW TO READ THOSE ANCHORS.** They are **RETAIL colocation prices** — what Psychz *charges* a colocation customer — and therefore **already contain Psychz's own margin. They are an upper bound on internal cost, not internal cost.** Applied naively they would overstate opex badly: a 400 W dedicated server allocated at retail would absorb **~$58/mo of power plus ~$4.50/mo of rack space**, which against the **$48/mo E3-1230 v2 SKU** would imply **negative margin**. Since that SKU is demonstrably still sold, internal cost must sit **materially below retail**, and bandwidth must be **heavily oversubscribed** rather than provisioned at the $225/mo unmetered rate. **ASSUMPTION: internal cost runs at roughly 40–60% of these published retail rates** — this is what pins the deduction band to 35–55% rather than something higher.
- **FURTHER ASSUMPTIONS, all labelled as such, none researched.** **(1)** The **12 / 18 / 24-month payback horizons are test points, not observed company policy** — no evidence was found of what payback period this operator actually targets, and their willingness to run 2010–2012 silicon suggests their real horizon is **far longer than 24 months**, which would raise every ceiling substantially. **(2)** Ceilings are computed on the **RENEWAL price, not the discounted first-term promotional price**; several SKUs carry heavy first-term discounts (E3-1230 v2 at $9.60 then $48.00; E5-1650 v3 at $92.40 then $462.00), so blended first-year revenue and therefore the true 12-month ceiling is **LOWER** than shown for those SKUs. **(3)** **100% utilisation is assumed** — no vacancy discount beyond what is embedded in the opex band. **(4)** **No allowance is made for the cost of IPv4 addresses** assigned to each server, despite the 2023 /16 acquisition representing real deployed capital. **(5)** Ceilings are **hardware-only** and exclude sales, billing, licensing and G&A.

**What the gap means for pitching an integrated platform.**

**First, and bluntly: on CPU, self-assembly wins and it is not close.** A used-market build recovers its cost in **2.4 to 7 months** against this operator's own rack rates. An integrated new-build platform is **not competing for a $15,299 budget** on the Platinum 8270 SKU — it is competing against a **$1,515 pile of parts that already works**. Any pitch that opens with new-versus-new price-per-core will lose. Fifteen years of catalogue evidence — **Atom D525 from 2010 and Xeon E3-1230 v2 from 2012 still earning revenue in 2026** — says this buyer has already made this calculation many times and is entirely comfortable with it.

**Second, the arithmetic that used to make self-assembly unbeatable is inverting, and memory is the reason.** In the Silver 4214 build, memory is **72–76% of the priced BOM**; in the Platinum 8270 build it is **52%**. The CPUs — 52 Cascade Lake cores — **cost less than half of what the RAM costs.** And DDR4 ECC is in a documented **2026 supply crisis**, quoted at **$400–$1,000+ per new module** against the $198 used here, with refurbished supply tightening. **Every one of these roll-ups is a snapshot of a cost base that is actively rising underneath them.** Their entire fleet economics depend on cheap secondhand DDR4 that is becoming scarce. **That is the wedge, and it is a genuine one rather than a talking point:** a DDR5 platform bought new is measured against DDR4 that may cost more than DDR5 within the refresh window. **Model that comparison explicitly, using their own $298 and $1,159 SKUs as the revenue side.**

**Third, and most important, there is exactly one part of their business where self-assembly is structurally impossible.** They already sell **H100 80GB SXM**. SXM5 modules are **not sold loose** — they ship only through NVIDIA-certified OEM partners as **complete HGX systems**, at **$250,000–$320,000 for an 8-GPU node ($31,000–$40,000 per GPU)**. No amount of eBay sourcing skill substitutes for a certified HGX baseboard. Note what their own catalogue then says: **the highest-revenue SKU they publish supports about $15,300 of hardware at a 24-month payback, which is under half the cost of a single H100 SXM module. Their entire priced CPU catalogue cannot fund one GPU.** That is why the GPU line is quote-only, unpriced and unstocked while every CPU SKU carries a price and an `in_stock` flag — **GPUs are bought against a signed customer order, not held as inventory.**

**So the pitch is not components and it is not price-per-core. It is the integrated HGX/SXM platform**, sold into a buyer who is already a channel buyer for that exact class of hardware, who has **no self-build alternative** on it, and whose binding constraint is **working-capital timing between customer signature and customer cash** rather than unit price. Bring **delivery certainty, staged delivery, and a credit or PO structure**, and the deal turns on **terms rather than discount**. Keep the **H100 94GB NVL** variant in view as the control case — that one is PCIe and they can and will self-integrate it, **which is precisely why it should not be the centre of the proposal.**

**Open GAPs in this section**

- The **operating-cost deduction (35–55%, midpoint 45%) driving every ceiling figure is an ASSUMPTION**, not a researched fact. Psychz is private, files no public accounts, and publishes no cost or margin data. **±10 points of deduction moves all ceilings by roughly ±18%.**
- The **12 / 18 / 24-month payback horizons are test points, not observed company policy.** No evidence of this operator's actual target payback was found, and their willingness to monetise 2010–2012 silicon strongly suggests a real horizon **well beyond 24 months**, which would raise every ceiling materially.
- **Ceilings are computed on renewal prices.** Several SKUs carry heavy first-term promotional discounts (E3-1230 v2 $9.60 → $48.00; E5-1650 v3 $92.40 → $462.00; Dual Silver 4214 $118.40 → $298.00), so true blended first-year revenue — and the true 12-month ceiling — is **LOWER** than stated for those SKUs. **The length of the promotional term was not established.**
- **NOT PRICED, no source retrieved:** 500 GB SATA enterprise SSD; 1 TB enterprise HDD; 1U single-socket LGA1151 barebone chassis/PSU; 40 GbE server network adapter. **All assembled-cost figures are therefore understatements.**
- The **32 GB memory price used for the E3-1270 v6 build is ECC RDIMM, but that platform requires UNBUFFERED ECC UDIMM.** The sourced price is indicative of the 32 GB ECC tier only and **is not a valid part for that socket**; the E3-1270 v6 roll-up is consequently reported as unusable rather than as a number.
- The **$149.99 chassis price is for a refurbished 2U barebone surfaced via eBay Refurbished, but the linked listing is an X10-generation (LGA2011-v3/v4) unit**, not the LGA3647 barebone the Silver 4214 and Platinum 8270 builds require. An exact LGA3647 barebone at a confirmed price was not obtained, and the Platinum 8270 build additionally needs a barebone **rated for 2 × 205 W TDP**, which commands a premium not captured here.
- **No assembly labour, burn-in, OS/licensing, rails, cabling, IPMI configuration, spares/RMA pool, or freight** is included in any roll-up. These are real costs in a self-assembly model and their omission **further understates assembled cost**.
- **The DDR4 pricing environment is unstable and the two sourced figures disagree by an order of magnitude** ($198/module from a retailer versus $400–$1,000+/module quoted as the March 2026 market). Which figure applies depends on new-versus-refurbished sourcing and on volume, **neither of which was established for this buyer**. This materially affects both roll-ups, since **memory is 52–76% of priced BOM**.
- **No BOM was constructed for a GPU SKU**, because Psychz publishes **no GPU price at all** (quote-only). The GPU cost side uses **market pricing** for H100 SXM ($25,000–$40,000 per module; $250,000–$320,000 per 8-GPU HGX node), **not Psychz's own economics**, so **no rent-derived GPU ceiling could be computed**.

## 14. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **high-probability existing / quasi-existing account → incumbent defence + generational refresh + GPU line extension**

**Not** a greenfield play, **not** a displacement play, and **not** a platform conversion — but also **not** an account you may address as a confirmed customer. Supermicro here is graded **circumstantial**, and the correct move is to convert the inference into a question rather than assert it.

**The honest wedge, in three parts:**

1. **The Supermicro read is strong but must stay a hypothesis.** They write and host **Supermicro-specific** IPMI documentation on their own support domain, there is **no iDRAC or iLO content anywhere on the site**, and their inventory carries a **Core i7-7700K server SKU that Dell and HPE simply do not build**. That is enough to walk in with a well-founded working assumption — and **not** enough to say "we know you're running Supermicro." Say it as a question. If it lands, the entire call reframes from cold intro to incumbent review.

2. **The refresh cycle is already running and is visible in their own document.** Their reference architecture names **3rd, 4th and 5th Gen Intel Xeon Scalable** — Ice Lake, Sapphire Rapids, Emerald Rapids — while the self-serve catalogue still sells **E3-1230 v2 (2012) through dual Platinum 8270 (2019)**. There is a **7-to-14-year legacy tail on the price list and a current-generation deployment happening quote-side.** That is a refresh conversation with a documented starting point, not a speculative one.

3. **The GPU gap is the strongest near-term hook.** They are **marketing H100 (80 GB SXM / 94 GB NVL), H200 141 GB, B200 and B300** with up-to-8-GPU systems — **and there is no configurator, no published price and no named platform vendor**, while their **CPU line is fully priced and self-serve**. That asymmetry says they are **sourcing GPU nodes deal-by-deal with no standing supply agreement**. The offer that fits: **turn 4-GPU and 8-GPU platforms into standard SKUs they can list and price**, and use the **per-kW single-GPU colocation option they already sell** as the density on-ramp. Their LA cabinets are licensed at **1.92–4.99 kW with up to 10 kW supported**, so density planning has to be part of the proposal, not an afterthought.

**Supporting signals worth naming on the call:** multiple inventory SKUs are **out of stock with waiting lists**, and a customer publicly reported a **3-week provisioning delay attributed to being "out of hard drives"** (*community-sourced, unverified*) — supply-constraint evidence that a hardware supplier can act on.

### The qualifying question to ask on first contact

> **"Are your H100 / H200 / B200 nodes currently sourced deal-by-deal? If you had a 4-GPU and an 8-GPU standard platform you could list and price directly, would you want to turn that into a standing SKU?"**

Ask that **one** question. Do not quote. Do not assume an incumbent vendor. If the conversation opens up, the natural second question is the platform one — *"which barebones or motherboard are your current 1U single-socket and 2U dual-socket builds on?"* — which is what settles the Supermicro grade for good.

**Best-placed contact:** **Ron K, VP of Product**, is the most likely technical buying-committee member. **William H. Lu** is CEO, CFO, Director and registered agent — in a company this closely held, he is the decision-maker for any capital commitment. **No individual executive email addresses are public**; route via **sales@psychz.net** or LinkedIn.

**Psychz runs a sizeable fleet — a 15,000 sq ft self-operated LA facility, 16–17 interconnection facilities, 188,928–283,904 announced IPv4 addresses, a claimed 6 Tbps+ network — and has never once appeared in the CRM** (verified 2026-08-03: no lead, no account, no DNC). **Under Rule 8, a distributor may cross territory boundaries, but this requires advance approval and a do-not-call flag.** Before anyone dials: **confirm with the channel desk whether Psychz is already an existing account of a distributor.** If it is, this stops being new-customer development and becomes a **distributor co-managed existing account** — different opening, different quoting, different registration path.
