## 1. Bottom line

WebNX, Inc. is a privately held, founder-led dedicated-server, colocation and GPU/AI hosting provider headquartered inside its own flagship datacenter at 119 North 600 West, Bldg 3B, Ogden, Utah, with leased footprints in Los Angeles (Equinix LA3), New York, Salt Lake City and Dallas, running AS18450 and self-describing "thousands of high-end servers globally" on a staff of 11–50 ([LinkedIn company page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/webnx-inc-), self-reported; [BBB profile](https://www.bbb.org/us/ut/ogden/profile/internet-service/webnx-inc-1166-90026506), file opened 2020-06-03). Commercially this is a **platform-conversion target, not an incumbent-defence account** — WebNX is a confirmed component-level whitebox integrator that has never named a system vendor anywhere in public, sits inside Team 1's own territory, and has never once appeared in the CRM. The single strongest piece of evidence is WebNX's own publicly priced flagship AI SKU — **dual AMD EPYC 9575F (128C/256T), 1.5 TB DDR5, 2 × 15 TB Gen5 NVMe, 4 × NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 96 GB at $7,999/month** ([Ogden GPU & AI store](https://clients.webnx.com/index.php?rp=/store/ogden-gpu-and-ai-servers), read 2026-08-03) — which proves they already buy and resell current-generation Turin + Blackwell platforms, while the same catalogue still carries 2017-silicon V100s and consumer RTX 3090s with **zero H100 / H200 / L40S coverage**. The one thing that could kill the deal is the channel: a fleet this size with no CRM record at all points at purchase through distribution, so under Rule 8 the distributor position must be cleared *before* the first call, or the engagement becomes a channel conflict rather than a sale.

## 11. Supermicro sales angle

### Classification: **Whitebox integrator → platform conversion**

**This is NOT an incumbent-defence account.** Supermicro's presence in the WebNX fleet is graded **unverified** — no chassis, no motherboard, no system-vendor name appears anywhere in WebNX's public material. **Opening with "we know you're running Supermicro" will destroy credibility on the first sentence.** Turn it into a question instead.

**The honest wedge:**

WebNX buys at the **component and barebones level** and integrates in-house — that is confirmed by its own job posting ("assembling & disassembling servers, testing hardware," "repair or replace faulty hardware components," "hundreds of server configurations and parts each year," [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/viewjob?jk=9329bfea43788efd)). And they have **already proven they will buy top-end current-generation AI platforms**: dual EPYC 9575F + 4 × RTX PRO 6000 96 GB, publicly listed at **$7,999/month** ([Ogden GPU & AI store](https://clients.webnx.com/index.php?rp=/store/ogden-gpu-and-ai-servers)). Yet the same catalogue still carries **2017-era V100** and **2020 consumer RTX 3090** nodes, and offers **no H100, H200, L40S or GB200 at all**.

The angle: **GPU-optimised 4U/5U barebones for V100 and RTX 3090 replacement, plus additional dual-Turin 4-GPU and 8-GPU platforms.** There is also a natural product fit that Dell and HPE structurally cannot match — WebNX sells **AM5 Ryzen 9950X/9950X3D and LGA1851 Core Ultra 9 285K** platforms, and Dell and HPE do not build servers around those desktop-class sockets while Supermicro does.

Secondary hooks worth holding in reserve:
- The **full-rack colocation SKU ($1,199/mo) publishes no amperage allocation**, and no kW-per-rack density or PUE exists publicly — density is an unforced discovery topic that leads naturally into AI-platform sizing.
- The **April 2021 generator fire** during the Ogden city-wide outage is a legitimate resiliency conversation and a plausible driver of power-plant and refresh capex.
- **DGX Spark units are already being paired at 200 Gbps** — they are building small multi-node AI clusters and will hit interconnect and density limits.

### The one qualifying question for first contact

> **"For your AM5 and LGA1851 SKUs and your 4-GPU boxes — whose barebones or motherboards are you building on today?"**

Ask this and nothing else on the first call. **Do not quote. Do not assume an incumbent vendor.** One sentence resolves the highest-priority unknown in this account: whether Supermicro is already in WebNX's supply chain at all.

### Rule 8 — distributor caution (read before dialling)

WebNX runs a sizeable fleet — self-reported "thousands of high-end servers globally", 150+ racks in Equinix LA3, a 100,000+ sq ft self-operated Ogden facility, ~100,096 IPv4 addresses on AS18450 — **yet has never once appeared in the CRM** (salesleads Search, Type = All, verified 2026-08-03: no lead, no account, no do-not-call). The only reasonable inference is that **their servers are bought through distribution, not direct**. Corroborating behaviour: the job posting describes moving "pallets full of servers or other computer parts with a pallet jack" and testing "hundreds of server configurations and parts each year"; LowEndTalk carries threads titled "Regarding WebNX distributors."

**Under Rule 8 a distributor may cross territory boundaries, but this requires advance approval and a do-not-call flag.** Therefore, before anyone dials: **confirm with the channel contact whether WebNX is already an existing account of a distributor.** If it is, this shifts from new-business development to a distributor-managed existing account, and the opening, the quoting route and the registration method all change.

**Sequence — do not reorder:** ① clear the channel/distributor position (Rule 8) → ② register the lead (Ogden UT = West Coast South excl. CA = **T1 | T31**, Team 1's own territory, direct registration) → ③ make contact, asking only the qualifying question above.
