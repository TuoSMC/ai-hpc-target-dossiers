1. Bottom line

Aunalytics, Inc. is a privately held, sponsor-backed data-and-AI platform operator headquartered at 460 Stull Street, South Bend, Indiana, running four ASNs — AS27287, AS36443, AS54069, AS54276 — out of two data centers it says it owns and operates itself (South Bend and Kalamazoo), plus leased network presence at 123.Net DC1 in Southfield, Michigan, Global Access Point Union Station in South Bend and Cologix COL2 in Columbus, Ohio, on a self-reported staff of ~300 (aunalytics.com/enterprise-cloud, read on this research pass). Commercially this is a direct end-buyer, not a channel partner and not a reseller — the "own published server catalogue and pricing" workstream returned nothing at all, and that is a fact about their business model, not a research failure: they do not sell servers, they buy servers for their own floor. The single strongest piece of evidence is not the widely-quoted April 2024 $30M equipment announcement (WNDU, 2024-04-19) but their own dated May 2026 architecture essays, published under named authorship, arguing that frontier GPU capacity is "constrained, and the constraint is physical", that frontier models are "economically inappropriate for most enterprise tasks", and that the right answer is an Intelligence Layer that routes each request to a specialized, classical-ML or deterministic tier the company controls, for data-residency reasons a bank regulator will enforce (aunalytics.com, 2026-05-11 and 2026-05-14). That is a written, dated, public commitment to buying and operating their own inference silicon. The thing that most likely kills the deal is arithmetic, not competition: §10 shows a $30M programme needs roughly $2.27M of incremental monthly revenue to clear a 24-month payback at a mid operating-cost assumption, sold into community banks and credit unions. Their strategy demands more nodes; their P&L demands cheaper nodes. That gap is the entire opening — and the one thing that must not happen is an attack on their NetApp storage estate, which is a proof point to regulated buyers, not a soft target.

10. Cost ceiling

What the equipment programme has to be worth to Aunalytics, and what a node can therefore cost. This section answers one question: at what hardware cost does the $30M programme still pay for itself?

10.1 Assumptions — read these first, they are assumptions and not findings

Honest caveat before anything else: the intended rent- or price-derived ceiling CANNOT BE BUILT for this account. Aunalytics publishes no rack rent, no GPU-hour rate, no colocation price and no SKU. The only pricing language on the entire site is qualitative: "Predictable pricing, with no ingress or egress charges and no hidden fees" (/enterprise-cloud/). A price-derived ceiling is substituted with a capex-envelope / payback derivation, and the substitution is stated here rather than hidden.

Method: Revenue required = Capex ÷ (1 − s), where s = operating-cost share of revenue.

  • ASSUMPTION, labelled and ranged: s = 35%–55%, mid 45%. This covers power, cooling, space, remote hands, transit and peering, software licence and support labour for a self-operated Midwest colocation plus managed-services provider. This is an analyst assumption, not an Aunalytics figure, and Aunalytics has published nothing that would test it.
  • Site asymmetry — real, but not modelled. South Bend and Kalamazoo are self-operated, so opex there should sit at the low end of the band; the Southfield, Union Station and Cologix presences are leased and belong at the high end. A single s is applied across the programme for comparability.
  • What is excluded. No cost of capital or discounting (a 24-month payback at a realistic 10–12% cost of capital is ~8–10% more demanding than the undiscounted figure shown); no sales, support or overhead allocation; no credit for the ~$2.5M equipment abatement, which if applied against the hardware line would materially raise the affordable node price; no residual value.
  • Payback-window choice. 12 / 18 / 24 months. Read the 24-month column as "the level at which this programme is comfortably self-funding", not "the level above which it is impossible".

Method to complete this BOM, stated so it can be executed the moment the inputs arrive:

  1. Obtain the incumbent node spec — system model, CPU generation, GPU model and count, RAM, NIC — from the customer directly, or from a filed equipment schedule attached to a UCC-1 (§8).
  2. Price that configuration at street from a reseller quote for the incumbent platform.
  3. Configure the matching Supermicro GPU system — a 4U/8U PCIe or SXM chassis with equivalent GPU count and matched CPU / RAM / NVMe / NIC — and price it list-less-partner-discount.
  4. Compare on delivered $/GPU and $/rack-U, never on headline chassis price.

Inputs 1 and 2 are the blockers. Both are GAP.

One independent BOM lever that does not need their SKU: FlexPod couples compute to a joint reference design that includes the NetApp storage estate. A Supermicro proposal therefore has to be framed as an additive GPU/inference tier that leaves the NetApp estate intact — not a FlexPod replacement. Attacking the storage layer loses the deal, because NetApp plus the 99.999% claim is what they sell to regulated buyers.

10.4 Roll-up — and what the gap means for the pitch

HEADLINE: at the mid assumption and a 24-month payback, the $30M programme has to throw off roughly $2.27M of incremental monthly revenue. For a ~300-person regional provider selling into community banks and credit unions, that is a demanding number — and it is arithmetic they can check themselves.

  1. Every dollar off the node price moves the payback date directly. That is the entire Supermicro argument, and unlike a speed-and-feeds pitch it survives contact with a CFO.
  2. Their own strategy is the pressure. Three 2026 essays argue for more owned inference capacity. §10.2 says that capacity has to clear $2.27M/month. Their strategy demands more nodes; their P&L demands cheaper nodes.
  3. The abatement is a lever nobody has used. ~$2.5M of city-council tax relief is attached specifically to advanced computing equipment. Ask how much remains unused; if a material amount does, the effective ceiling per node rises and the payback shortens without a single price concession.
  4. Do not lead with integration savings. They have ~300 staff, two self-operated data centers and a two-person network team — the constraint is operability and capital, not assembly labour.
  5. Expect a structure conversation as well as a price conversation. With $21.5M of a $30M programme undrawn on the public record (§9.4) and the lien position unknown (§8), whether structured terms are even available is unresolved — which is why the free Indiana UCC browse has to happen before terms are quoted.
  6. The honest limit of this model: it is anchored on two confirmed capital figures and one assumed operating-cost band. It does not know their revenue, their margin or their billable rate per node. Present it as a shared arithmetic exercise, not as a finding about their business — and let them correct the inputs. Getting corrected is the goal; a corrected model hands you the per-node ceiling that §10.2 is missing.

14. Supermicro sales angle

Classification: Vertically integrated end-buyer → additive inference tier

This is NOT a channel-partner account and NOT an incumbent-defence account. They own the buildings, they own four ASNs, they buy compute for their own floor, and no system vendor is named anywhere in their public material — the only vendor they publish about themselves is NetApp, on storage. Opening with "we know you're running Supermicro" — or "Cisco", or "Dell" — will destroy credibility on the first sentence. Turn it into a question instead.

The honest wedge: their own May 2026 essay, quoted back to them.

They have published, under their own name and with a date, that (1) frontier GPU capacity is physically constrained, (2) most enterprise inference should never touch a frontier endpoint, (3) regulated customers require the work to happen inside an environment they control, and (4) the durable asset is the governed memory layer, not the model. Every one of those four points is an argument for buying more owned inference silicon, cheaper, sooner. Supermicro sells exactly that.

At the same time the arithmetic in §10 is unforgiving: a $30M equipment programme needs roughly $2.27M/month of incremental revenue to clear a 24-month payback at the mid operating-cost assumption. Their sales motion is community banks and credit unions — a good business, but not one that absorbs hyperscaler-priced hardware. Their strategy demands more nodes; their P&L demands cheaper nodes. That gap is the opening.

Three things make this a real deal rather than a hopeful one:

  1. They own the buildings. No landlord, no hyperscaler and no reseller sits inside the decision. 1440 Ignition Dr and 6395 Technology Ave are theirs to configure.
  2. They have already spent municipal money earmarked for advanced computing equipment, and taken a job-creation commitment against it. Underdelivering on the build carries a political cost in South Bend, not merely a commercial one.
  3. Their network estate is stale relative to their AI rhetoric — the last declared physical-footprint change is 2024-09-23, while three 2026 essays argue for expansion.

What not to do — this is the part most likely to be got wrong:

  • Do not attack FlexPod or NetApp. The storage estate and the 99.999% uptime claim are their proof points to regulated buyers. Position Supermicro as the additive inference tier that the Intelligence Layer routes to — the "specialized, classical ML, deterministic" tiers in their own taxonomy — sitting alongside, not instead of, the existing platform.
  • Do not state any incumbent hardware vendor as fact. Cisco UCS is inferred from an unverified FlexPod reference, nothing more (§6).
  • Do not raise politics. §12 shows a bipartisan civic giving pattern with nothing to leverage.
  • Do not approach Lexington, Kentucky. NetGain Technologies is a different company owned by Lyra Technology Group (§3, row 1).

The one qualifying question for first contact

"Your May essay said the right architecture routes most work away from frontier endpoints, to specialized and classical tiers you control. What are you running those tiers on today — and what does a node have to cost, per month, for the routing decision to pencil out against a frontier API call?"

Ask this and little else on the first call. It is their own thesis, so it cannot be dismissed as a vendor pitch; it forces the $/node number that §10.2 is missing; and it cannot be answered with "we use the cloud" — because their own published architecture says they must not.

Send it to David Cieslak. Not to Tracy Graham.

10. 成本天花板

那套設備計畫對 Aunalytics 必須值多少，以及節點因此可以貴到多少。本節只回答一個問題：在什麼硬體成本之下，3,000 萬美元的計畫仍然付得起自己？

10.1 假設——請先讀，這些是假設，不是查證結果

先講最誠實的一句：本案的「由租金或售價推導的天花板」根本無法建立。 Aunalytics 不公布機櫃租金、不公布 GPU 小時費率、不公布主機代管價格、不公布任何 SKU。全站唯一與定價相關的文字是定性的："Predictable pricing, with no ingress or egress charges and no hidden fees"（/enterprise-cloud/）。因此改以「資本額度／回收期」推導，而這個替代作法在此明白說出，不藏起來。

方法：所需營收 = 資本支出 ÷ (1 − s)，其中 s ＝營運成本占營收比重。

  • 假設，已標示且給區間：s = 35%–55%，中位 45%。 涵蓋自營中西部主機代管兼代管服務業者的電力、冷卻、空間、現場人力、傳輸與 peering、軟體授權與支援人力。這是分析假設，不是 Aunalytics 的數字，而且 Aunalytics 未公布任何可用來檢驗它的資料。
  • 站點不對稱——真實存在，但未建模。 South Bend 與 Kalamazoo 為自營，其 opex 應落在區間低端；Southfield、Union Station 與 Cologix 為租用，應落在高端。為了可比性，全計畫套用單一 s。
  • 未計入者。 未計資金成本或折現（在合理的 10–12% 資金成本下，24 個月回收比所示未折現數字再嚴苛約 8–10%）；未分攤業務、支援與管銷；未計入約 250 萬美元的設備減免，若把它抵在硬體項目上，可負擔的節點單價會明顯提高；未計殘值。
  • 回收期選擇。 12／18／24 個月。24 個月那一欄請讀成「這個計畫可以舒服地自我支撐的水準」，不是「超過這個水準就不可能」。

10.3 物料成本（BOM）——不捏造數字就無法定價

Aunalytics 不公布任何 SKU、不具名任何伺服器供應商。本檔不主張任何 BOM、不引用任何街價、不重建任何規格表。 捏造其中任何一項，會是這份檔案能做的最有殺傷力的事——因為第一通技術電話就會拆穿它。

關於其平台，紀錄上確實存在的內容，逐字取自 /enterprise-cloud/：

  • "advanced GPU and data-caching services to analyze massive volumes of transaction data"
  • "NetApp SSD and NVMe storage with redundant fiber connectivity for low-latency access"
  • "We own and operate our infrastructure and data centers"
  • "99.999% Uptime across Aunalytics Cloud and ISP services over the past 7 years" 對比 "Typical hyperscaler SLA ~99.9%"
  • "~300 team members, including data scientists, engineers, and cloud specialists"

再加上來自先前工作底稿、等級為旁證者：各站採 FlexPod 參考架構搭配 NetApp SSD／NVMe，站點間為冗餘 10 Gig 光纖。

完成此 BOM 的方法，在此明列，以便輸入一到位即可執行：

  1. 直接向客戶取得現行節點規格——系統型號、CPU 世代、GPU 型號與數量、記憶體、網卡——或自 UCC-1 所附之設備清單取得（第 8 節）。
  2. 以該組態向現行平台的經銷商取得街價報價。
  3. 配置對應的 Supermicro GPU 系統——GPU 數量相當、CPU／記憶體／NVMe／網卡對齊的 4U/8U PCIe 或 SXM 機殼——並以牌價扣除夥伴折扣定價。
  4. 以交付後的 $/GPU 與 $/機櫃 U 數比較，絕不比機殼牌價。

輸入 1 與 2 是卡點，兩者皆為 GAP。

一個不需要他們 SKU 就成立的獨立 BOM 槓桿： FlexPod 把運算與一套包含 NetApp 儲存體系的聯合參考設計綁在一起。因此 Supermicro 的提案必須被框成保留 NetApp 體系完整、額外增加的 GPU／推論層，而不是 FlexPod 的置換。攻擊儲存層＝輸掉這筆生意，因為 NetApp 加上 99.999% 的說法，正是他們賣給受監理客戶的東西。

10.4 彙總——這個落差對提案的意義

結論：在中位假設與 24 個月回收之下，3,000 萬美元的計畫必須產出每月約 227 萬美元的增額營收。對一家約 300 人、主攻社區銀行與信用合作社的區域型業者而言，這是一個吃力的數字——而且這是他們自己就能驗算的算術。

  1. 節點單價每少一塊，回收日期就直接往前一格。 這就是 Supermicro 的全部論證，而且與規格數字式的提案不同，它禁得起 CFO 的檢視。
  2. 壓力來自他們自己的策略。 三篇 2026 年文章論證要有更多自有推論產能。10.2 說那些產能必須跨過每月 227 萬美元。他們的策略要求更多節點；他們的損益表要求更便宜的節點。
  3. 那筆減免是一個沒人用過的槓桿。 約 250 萬美元的市議會稅務減免專門針對先進運算設備。去問還剩多少未使用；若餘額可觀，每節點的實質天花板就會上升、回收期縮短，而且完全不需要任何價格讓步。
  4. 不要以「省下整合工時」開場。 他們有約 300 名員工、兩座自營資料中心、一支兩人網路團隊——瓶頸在可維運性與資本，不在組裝人力。
  5. 要預期這同時是一場結構的對話，不只是價格的對話。 3,000 萬中的 2,150 萬在公開紀錄上未動用（9.4），而質權狀態未知（第 8 節），因此「結構化條件是否可行」根本尚未確定——這就是為什麼在報價條件之前，必須先完成那筆免費的 Indiana UCC 查詢。
  6. 這個模型的誠實界線： 它建立在兩個已確認的資本數字與一個假設的營運成本區間上。它不知道他們的營收、毛利，或每節點計費費率。請把它當成一次共同的算術演練提出，而不是關於他們業務的定論——並讓他們修正輸入值。被修正就是目的；一個被修正過的模型，會直接交出 10.2 所缺的那個每節點天花板。

10.5 GAP — 第三軌，明白陳述

  • 35–55% 的營運成本區間是假設，不是查證結果。 它並非由 Aunalytics 的資料推得，那些資料一項都不公開。所有天花板數字在此區間內約有 ±17% 的變動。
  • 不存在每節點天花板，因為沒有公布任何每 GPU 節點或每租戶的計費費率。這是本檔中最有價值的缺項。
  • 未建立任何 BOM，也未引用任何街價。 兩項必要輸入——現行節點規格與可比報價——皆為 GAP。沒有用任何估計去填補這個洞。
  • 3,000 萬美元是一則公告，不是一份已申報的承諾。 它來自單一地方媒體（WNDU，2024-04-19），且描述的是分期計畫。實際已支出多少是 GAP。

  • 沒有任何營收數字可作分母做合理性檢核。 這個模型能說出計畫必須賺多少，但說不出那相當於公司的多少比例。

14. Supermicro 銷售切入點

分類：垂直整合終端買家 → 加法式推論層（additive inference tier）

本案不是通路夥伴案，也不是既有客戶防守案。 他們自有建物、自有四個 ASN、為自己的機房買運算，而且公開資料中沒有具名任何系統廠——他們唯一對外具名的供應商是儲存層的 NetApp。以「我們知道你們在用 Supermicro」——或「Cisco」、「Dell」——開場，第一句話就會失去可信度。改成問句。
