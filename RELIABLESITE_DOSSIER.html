1. Bottom line

ReliableSite is a founder-owned, ~11–50-person U.S. bare-metal / dedicated-server provider running its own AS23470 network and its own servers across six leased colocation regions — Piscataway NJ, Newark NJ, Miami FL, Los Angeles CA, Amsterdam NL, and a Querétaro MX build-out — selling single-socket desktop/workstation-class machines at $49–$699/month. The commercial verdict is that this is a confirmed Supermicro account that has already been half-lost: the chassis business is gone to an in-house design, but the motherboard and power-supply socket is explicitly still open, and there is a clean, empty AI/GPU shelf to fill — so this is a board-level defence plus a greenfield GPU play, not a routine renewal, and it must be run by the East Coast team, not Team 1. The single strongest piece of evidence in the whole file is CEO Radic Davydov's on-record LowEndBox interview, in which he names Supermicro MicroCloud in his fleet and then explains, in his own words, that he designed a proprietary ~50%-higher-density chassis after MicroCloud replacement parts became unavailable and cost "2-3 times the cost" — while deliberately keeping it compatible with off-the-shelf motherboards and power supplies. The one thing that could kill the deal is opening with a vendor-integrated chassis or complete-system pitch: that re-opens the exact wound that lost the account, and the honest opening is the board/PSU socket and the missing GPU platform — the motherboard supplier behind the custom chassis is the single most commercially important unknown in this entire dossier.

11. Supermicro sales angle

Classification: confirmed incumbent — but in practice this is a chassis-loss defence in which the board-level socket is still open, plus a greenfield AI/GPU play.

This is not a routine renewal and it is not a cold displacement. Supermicro is genuinely in this fleet, confirmed by three independent strands (§6). But the same CEO interview that confirms it is also the on-record account of why they walked away from the vendor chassis: MicroCloud replacement parts became unavailable, near-identical newer-generation parts would not fit, and the price was "2-3 times the cost", so he built his own ~50%-denser chassis — while deliberately keeping it compatible with off-the-shelf motherboards and power supplies.

That single design decision defines the entire honest wedge:

  1. Board and PSU, not chassis. The chassis business is gone and should not be re-pitched first — re-opening it re-opens the grievance. What is still open, by the CEO's own published design intent, is the motherboard and power supply socket. Who supplies the boards for the custom chassis is the single most commercially important unknown in this dossier, and it is unanswerable from public sources.
  2. Parts availability and lead time is a live, first-hand pain point. The CEO publicly stated an "800% cost increase" on specific SSD/RAM parts with 3+ month lead times (LowEndTalk). Long-life part availability and a stable supply commitment speak directly to the reason he left in the first place.
  3. The AI gap is real and published. They market "GPU AI Dedicated Servers", position the EPYC 4545P for AI inferencing, and publish an AI-vision customer case study — with one A10 (QTY 1) and a $99/month Quadro RTX 4000 add-on behind it. A 4-GPU L40S / RTX PRO class node they can list, price and self-serve is the natural first GPU SKU, sized to a 1–2 rack single-socket operation, not an HGX/SXM scale-out pitch.
  4. They buy platforms Dell and HPE do not build. The fleet is single-socket desktop/workstation silicon — AM5 Ryzen 9950X, LGA1700 i9 14900K, Threadripper 7995WX, EPYC 4545P. That is a structural product fit, and it is why there is no Dell or HPE incumbency to displace (both = no evidence found).
  5. Querétaro is the timing trigger. The QRO region is publicly "coming soon" with the network test not yet live — a build-out in progress is the most likely near-term new-hardware purchase event in the account.

"What motherboard and power supply are you using in your own chassis — and what platform is going into Querétaro?"

11. Supermicro 銷售切入點

分類：已確認既有客戶——但實際上是「機殼已流失、板級仍開放」的防守戰，外加 GPU 全新開發。

這不是例行續約，也不是冷啟動的替換戰。Supermicro 確實在這支機隊裡，由三條獨立證據確認（第 6 節）。但同一份確認它的 CEO 專訪，也正是他們 離開原廠機殼的第一手說明：MicroCloud 替換零件買不到、幾乎相同的新世代零件裝不上、價格是「2-3 times the cost」，於是他自建密度高約 50% 的機殼——並刻意讓它相容於市售主機板與電源。

這一個設計決定，界定了整個誠實的楔子：

  1. 打主機板與電源，不是打機殼。 機殼生意已經流失，不該優先重推——重推等於重新掀開舊帳。依 CEO 自己公布的設計意圖，仍然開放的是 主機板與電源這個插槽。自製機殼的主機板由誰供應，是本檔商業價值最高的單一未知，而且公開來源無法回答。
  2. 零件供應與交期是活的、第一手的痛點。 CEO 公開表示特定 SSD／RAM 零件出現 「800% cost increase」、交期 3 個月以上（LowEndTalk）。長生命週期的零件供應與穩定供貨承諾，正好直接回應當初讓他離開的理由。
  3. AI 缺口是真的，而且已經公布。 他們行銷「GPU AI Dedicated Servers」、把 EPYC 4545P 定位為 AI inferencing、還發布 AI 視覺客戶案例——背後卻只有 一款 A10（庫存 1 台）與一張每月 $99 的 Quadro RTX 4000 加購卡。一款他們能直接上架、標價、自助下單的 4 卡 L40S／RTX PRO 級節點，是最自然的第一個 GPU SKU，尺度對應 1–2 個機櫃、以單路為主的營運型態，而不是 HGX／SXM 的規模化提案。
  4. 他們買的是 Dell 與 HPE 根本不做的平台。 機隊是單路桌上型／工作站級矽晶——AM5 Ryzen 9950X、LGA1700 i9 14900K、Threadripper 7995WX、EPYC 4545P。這是結構性的產品契合，也正是為什麼這裡沒有 Dell 或 HPE 既有關係可以替換（兩者皆為查無證據）。
  5. Querétaro 是時間觸發點。 QRO 區域公開標示為「coming soon」、網路測試尚未開通——建置進行中，是本帳戶最可能的近期新硬體採購事件。

首通電話只問這一句，其他都不問。它一句話就解決本檔最高價值的未知；它承認自製機殼是他們的（尊重那個設計決定，而不是攻擊它）；它同時把建置中的區域帶出來。不報價。不假設既有廠商。不要用「我們知道你們在用 Supermicro」開場——這對機隊的歷史為真，但在當世代 SKU 級並未確認，而且 CEO 自己公開講過離開 MicroCloud 的經過，帶著佔有感的開場會踩到地雷。

規則 8 通路警語——撥出第一通電話前必讀

本業者明顯在營運相當規模的機隊——六個區域、約 50,944 個 IPv4 位址、五個 IXP 據點各 100G 埠、自報 1–5 Tbps——卻在 CRM 中一筆紀錄都沒有（2026-08-03 於 salesleads Search，Type = All 實查：無 lead、無 account、無 do-not-call）。合理的推論只有一個：他們的伺服器是透過經銷通路買的，不是直接向原廠買。 佐證是他們自述的採購型態：現場備料「keep our inventory on site」、「from tray to rack」自行上架——這正是以零件與準系統為主、透過通路夥伴進貨的行為模式。

依規則 8，經銷商可以跨越轄區邊界，但必須事先取得核准並掛上「do not call」標記。 因此：先確認通路，再登錄 lead，最後才接觸。 在任何人撥出第一通電話之前，須先與通路窗口確認 ReliableSite 是否已是某家經銷商的既有帳戶。若是，本檔就從「新客開發」轉為「與經銷商共同經營的既有帳戶」，開場方式、報價路徑與登錄方式全部不同。順序顛倒會造成通路衝突。
